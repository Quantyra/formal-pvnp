# Affine feedback saturation: selected-rule source comparison

Date: 2026-09-11. S3057, E004/S008. Independent bounded source audit and mathematical check; no implementation, experiment, novelty or publication claim. README and INTEGRITY-CLAIMS boundaries apply.

## Selected operation

The author's rule keeps each residual clause as a disjunction of affine forms in current quotient coordinates. First canonicalize each clause through the augmented RREF of its complete falsifying equations in fixed quotient-coordinate order, retaining offsets and handling constants. For fixed w, enumerate spans generated by at most w nonzero coefficient rows from these canonical falsity systems, collect the clauses whose vectors lie in each span, and evaluate their conjunction on its at most 2^w assignments. Add every equation defining that local relation's affine hull, retain the residual clauses, substitute independent new equations, and repeat. Empty local relations certify inconsistency. Constants in affine forms must be retained during evaluation even though span eligibility uses linear parts.

This is a local truth-table calculation, not a global affine-hull oracle. At most n independent consistent equations can be learned, but this alone is not a work bound: scanning candidate spans, clause membership, table evaluation, canonicalization, and substitution must all be charged. For fixed w, enumerating at most L^w generating subsets of L occurring forms is a finite polynomial candidate scheme; repeated spans may be deduplicated. No parity-to-CNF expansion is needed. The residual clauses must remain unless an exact equivalence justifies deleting them: replacing a non-affine relation by its affine hull generally introduces spurious solutions.

## Primary overlap, checked before the derivation

Section 4 includes Gaussian Constraint Propagation (Proposition 4.8), learning linear consequences (4.10), and strongly connected component processing (4.12–4.13), followed by a DPLL solver (4.24). Thus compact affine clauses and iterative linear feedback are established methods; two affine forms per clause do not imply tractability. The SCC step is a directly relevant stronger comparator for our cycles. This audit does not establish that their algorithm is identical to the selected all-bounded-span truth-table rule.

**Beame and Sun, [Extending CDCL to Disjunctions of Parity Equations](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5), SAT 2026, published July 16.** The proceedings PDF's Section 2, Definitions 2.1–2.2 (pp. 5:3–4), describes linear clauses and their semantic representation by the span of negated equations. Introduction pp. 5:2–3 states linear-algebraic propagation, parity decisions and learned linear clauses; polynomial simulation of Res(parity) requires nondeterministic decisions and restarts. It is not a deterministic polynomial SAT algorithm. The paper identifies concurrent Danner–Kreuzer conflict-driven XNF work. Our local saturation limitation does not transfer to these stronger systems.

## Actual falsifiable limitation: independent check

For every fixed w>=2, use two directed implication cycles of length w+1 sharing x:

`x => a1 => ... => aw => NOT x`,

`NOT x => b1 => ... => bw => x`.

The first chain forces x=0 and the second forces x=1, so their conjunction is UNSAT. Initially all coefficient vectors are coordinate unit vectors. Every allowed rank-at-most-w span is therefore a coordinate window of at most w variables. Its collected binary clauses form a forest: neither complete cycle fits, and the two cycles meet only at x.

For any such forest and any vertex v, choose each neighbor's value to satisfy the incident edge regardless of v. This is possible because each edge forbids exactly one assignment pair. Extend these choices into the disjoint remaining trees. There are then two satisfying assignments differing only at v. The differences of satisfying assignments consequently span every coordinate direction, so the local satisfying relation has full affine hull. Isolated variables obey the same argument. Every permitted window is satisfiable and yields no nontrivial equation. The first pass stalls; further identical passes cannot help.

At window size w+1 each complete cycle is visible and its affine hull contains its forced root equation; the two equations conflict. This checks the precise window limitation. It does not prove a universal lower bound for bounded-width CSP algorithms, ordinary implication propagation, all XNF inference, SAT decision, or quantum algorithms. Standard 2-SAT SCC/reachability reasoning already detects the contradiction without these windows.

## Interpretation and useful distinction

The operation generalizes the earlier paired-path substitution repair operationally, but its basic representation and feedback principle are known. The new local exercise tests one explicitly defined saturation rule and exhibits information it discards: globally composable implications can survive every allowed window while contributing no local affine equation. Its proof is elementary; originality of this exact formulation has not been established.

A possible next change would preserve and compose non-affine residual information such as implications, rather than merely increasing w or repeating a fixed point. On binary clauses that is established 2-SAT/XNF SCC machinery. Any further discovery claim would therefore need a new explicit rule and a family beyond this known case, with a total representation/work bound. This note neither launches that further attempt nor presents the present counterexample as publishable novelty.

## Generalized family convention in the final derivation

The [final derivation](2026-09-11-affine-feedback-mechanism.md) names the two-cycle formula F_ell, with ell>=3 vertices per cycle and 2ell-1 total variables. The same forest argument proves stalling for every w<ell and detection at w=ell. Thus each fixed w has arbitrarily large failing examples, not just the single adjacent case ell=w+1. This is the same elementary proof with an independent family-size parameter, not a new primary-source claim. The implemented scope generators are specifically canonical clause-falsity RREF rows, and constants are handled before their enumeration.
