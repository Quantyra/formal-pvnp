## Independent checks completed

I read the S3074 exact indexed weighted-claw contract, S3075 source selection, and the independent S3076 complexity note. I directly opened the [official SAC 2020 primary paper](https://sacworkshop.org/SAC20/files/preproceedings/04-QuantumSearch.pdf), Problem 2.1 and Appendix B, and [Aldous-Miermont-Pitman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401115), equation (1) and the introduction's scaling assumptions.

For a fixed partition F of an N-element index domain into q nonempty fibers, with independent uniform values R(z) in that index domain, every positive-length predecessor path into x uses an edge whose image is x. Thus, if two distinct fixed indices x,y each have a predecessor other than themselves, both x and y occur among the q values R(z). A union bound over ordered distinct fiber pairs bounds this event by q(q-1)/N^2. This independently verifies the complexity reviewer's consultation claim. The source's predecessor count includes the zero-step endpoint, so a statement in that convention needs thresholds at least two. It is only a necessary-event upper bound; it does not control aggregate accepted collisions, or starting subsets containing an endpoint without any inbound edge.

The class transition z -> F(R(z)) does have the independent p-mapping law with p(z')=|F^-1(z')|/N. This follows by counting the preimages of each destination class, independently for each source class. Original endpoint identity is lost on passing to this quotient. The primary p-mapping paper studies weak convergence under asymptotic mass assumptions; its inspected statements do not establish the finite-size, two-fixed-endpoint, truncated predecessor tail needed here.

## Source precision and cost boundaries

The SAC source counts zero-step predecessors. Its random-map tail, truncated-height requirement, sparse marked-subset regime, and post-merge distinguished-point reachability are separate obligations. Neither the existence of a useful pair nor large expected basin mass alone is a substitute. The displayed relative O(1/N) remainder in Lemma B.1 should not be treated as independently verified uniformly for growing t: equation (19) retains sqrt(N/(N-t)), whose relative correction can be of order t/N. Our local elementary bound does not require that expansion. This observation is a precision warning, not an audit or refutation of the published algorithm.

An ideal independent random map on q fibers is a distributional device, not a free polynomial-gate circuit. A compact family would need an explicit proof of the independence or approximation used throughout the adaptive/coherent computation. Classical lazy sampling by itself does not establish coherent implementation. No generic quantum lower bound or random-FKO impossibility follows from failure of this particular transfer.

## Preparation clarification and final verdict

Bucket preparation is close to a phase-adjusted uniform state, not necessarily the phase-zero vector. For each controlled bucket label, absorb that phase into the ideal coin isometry; it cancels from its projector and V*SV. Uniform state error on fresh zero coins then bounds the error of the associated rank-one projectors and reflections on arbitrary inputs. One must use that argument rather than assume the complete preparation unitary is close on every possible workspace state.
