## Source inputs and the important correction

Source: [OpenAI analytical manuscript](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/32d9f210-8b73-45e0-91bc-82a30aef8a9a/navier-stokes.pdf), accessed 2026-09-08. These supply smooth coefficient factorizations, zero positive-order axial/angular axis traces, arbitrary-order Cartesian tail control, inner equality with the background, and eventual equality after localization. The displayed annular estimate (5.42) alone does not reach the axis. The argument below derives the needed axis estimate from the stronger summation construction. It assumes the cited construction and its stated estimates; it is not an audit of that construction.

Use the notation of `2026-09-08-material-carrier-analysis.md`: A=1/2+h, D=1/2-h, d=1-eta^2, L=1-2h eta^2, tau=q d, z=q^D eta, r^2=2qX. Fix a small rectangle 0<=X<=b<Xa and eta near the leading equilibrium eta0. Here all completed annular corrections vanish. Define actual normalized background functions

    Uhat(q,X,eta) = q^A u_z,
    Fhat(q,X,eta) = q^(1+h) u_theta/r,
    Mhat(q,X,eta) = integral_0^1 Uhat(q,vX,eta) dv.

The angular definition equals E/sqrt(2X) at leading order. The exact streamfunction is S=q^(1-A) X Mhat. Differentiating this streamfunction, rather than substituting a q-dependent U into the old leading formula, gives

    (r u_r)/X = [2 eta Uhat - 2D eta Mhat
                 - 2 eta q partial_q Mhat - d partial_eta Mhat]/L.

The extra q derivative includes derivatives of all summation cutoffs. Omitting it would give the wrong completed parcel equation. In increasing time ds/dt=1/(qL), the exact inner material equations are

    q'   = q(-1+2 eta Uhat),
    eta' = D eta+d Uhat,
    X'   = X What,
    What = 1-2D eta Mhat-2 eta q partial_q Mhat-d partial_eta Mhat,
    theta' = L q^(-h) Fhat.                                      (1)

Primes here mean d/ds. These equations follow from the coordinate chain rule and incompressibility, not from a fixed-position sampling asymptotic.

## Uniform normalized estimates at the axis

For every fixed finite derivative composition T in q partial_q, partial_X, partial_eta, the summed construction implies, on the small closed rectangle,

    T(Uhat-U0)=O(q^(2h)),    T(Fhat-F0)=O(q^(2h)).                 (2)

Here is why the vanishing-radius divisions do not invalidate this inference. Write y=r/sqrt(q), so X=y^2/2, and evaluate the Cartesian velocity on (r,0,z). Its axial component is even in y, its second transverse component is odd. The normalized axial scalar is q^A times the former. The normalized angular scalar is q^A times the latter divided by y. For an odd smooth function f,

    f(y)/y = integral_0^1 f'(vy) dv.

For an even smooth function g, the same integral identity applied to g' controls g'(y)/y. Iterating controls k derivatives in X using finitely many transverse derivatives (2k for an even scalar and 2k+1 for an odd scalar divided by y). These estimates are uniform down to y=0. They do not divide a pointwise remainder bound by an uncontrolled y.

At fixed y,eta, the derivative q partial_q pulls back to

    (r/2) partial_r + D z partial_z - tau partial_t,

and partial_eta at fixed q,y pulls back to

    q^D partial_z + 2q eta partial_t.

Also partial_y=sqrt(q) partial_r. On the chosen rectangle these coefficients, and the finitely many coefficients generated by repeating the operations, have at most a fixed power loss in q; that loss does not depend on truncation order. The same is true after multiplying by q^A. Thus a physical Cartesian remainder estimate through sufficiently many derivatives supplies the corresponding normalized estimate, with a fixed loss only.

For a requested finite set of normalized derivatives, choose a Cartesian derivative order m large enough for the preceding conversion and let K be its fixed additional loss. Lemma 5.4/(5.35), printed p.58, bounds the background physical tail by 2^(-J) q^(h(J+1)-ell'_m) for J>=max(1,m) and 0<q<(2c_J)^(-1); here g_j=2jh and ell'_m is independent of J. Choose J so h(J+1)-ell'_m-K>=2h. The normalized tail is then O(q^(2h)). The finite positive-order block is O(q^(2h)) directly from its smooth coefficients, since its first power is q^(2h) and its cutoffs equal one for sufficiently small q. This proves (2). The constants and the threshold depend on derivative order and on J; no uniform bound in J or algorithm for computing those constants is claimed. Radial averaging preserves (2), so What-W0 has the same bounds.

There is also an exact statement, stronger than a remainder estimate. Local finiteness for each q>0 and the zero coefficient axis traces give

    Uhat(q,0,eta)=4eta+j0,
    Fhat(q,0,eta)=F0(0,eta),
    What(q,0,eta)=W0(0,eta).                                   (3)

The radial identity proves the last equality: Mhat has the same axis trace as Uhat, and its q derivative vanishes there. Uniform X derivative bounds further give departures from each of these axis values of O(X). Annular support exclusion must be used here; smoothness at each individual time alone would not imply it. The common lower boundary is the fixed Xa, not a q-dependent vanishing bound: use Proposition 7.2's shell support, the compact radial integration map in Section 8.2/(8.4), printed pp.89-90, and the support-preserving states of Definition 9.4. Multiplication of these potentials by functions of q cannot enlarge their X support.

## Stable sheet for the completed inner field

Put rho=q^h. Equation (2) says that each normalized correction is O(rho^2), with partial_rho correction O(rho), and with continuous limits of the needed X and eta derivatives. Consequently the (rho,X,eta) part of (1) extends as a C1 vector field to rho=0. Extend each correction as zero to rho<0; its first derivatives match. A C1 extension in X across X=0 is possible from the smooth one-sided coefficient factorizations. Thus an ordinary local C1 stable-manifold theorem applies to the extended field. No theorem about a merely continuous nonautonomous perturbation is being invoked.

Set d0=1-eta0^2, L0=1-2h eta0^2, k=L0/d0. Recall eta0 is the root of D eta+d(4eta+j0)=0. Write w=W0(0,eta0)<0 and a=partial_eta[D eta+d(4eta+j0)] at eta0>0. The eigenvalues of the extended field at (0,0,eta0) are

    -hk, w, a.

The two stable eigendirections project bijectively onto (rho,X). Its local stable manifold therefore is a graph

    eta=eta0+g(rho,X).

By (3), the line X=0, eta=eta0, rho>=0 is an exact stable orbit family. Uniqueness of the local stable set gives g(rho,0)=0. Since g is C1,

    |g(rho,X)| <= C X.                                        (4)

Choose rho0>0 and X0>0 sufficiently small and use the graph value for eta. Local stable-manifold invariance keeps the trajectory in the neighborhood, converging to the equilibrium. Both rho and X remain positive at every finite s because their equations have multiplicative factors and bounded coefficients there. This is a positive-radius material trajectory of the completed inner field, not an on-axis parcel.

From (3)-(4), uniformly along this trajectory,

    q'/q=-k+O(X),    X'/X=w+O(X).

First shrink the neighborhood to obtain X<=X0 exp(ws/2), with initial s=0. Integrating the O(X) terms, which are absolutely integrable, then yields positive constants Cq,CX with

    q(s)=Cq exp(-ks)(1+o(1)),
    X(s)=CX exp(ws)(1+o(1)).                                  (5)

The existence of positive limits follows by exponentiating the convergent integrals, not merely by taking logarithmic growth rates. Since eta->eta0 and tau=qd, physical time tends to exactly 1. The resulting r and z tend to zero. Proposition 10.1's fixed cutoff-one neighborhood contains the entire trajectory after choosing its initial time sufficiently late; hence the same trajectory belongs to the final localized flow.

Define beta=-w/k>0 and gamma=(1+beta)/2. Equations (4)-(5) imply

    q/tau -> 1/d0,
    X ~ C tau^beta,
    r ~ Cr tau^gamma,      eta-eta0=O(tau^beta).                (6)

This material-radius exponent is substantially different from the radius tau^(1/2) of a fixed-similarity sampling circle. With the parameter bounds in the earlier carrier calculation, k<1.001 and -w>2.8, hence beta>2.79>h and gamma>1.895 (approximately 2 for small j0,h). These conservative inequalities follow from |eta0|<j0/4<=0.0125 and 0<h<0.01; they do not require numerical root finding.

The axis swirl F0(0,eta0)>0. Using (3)-(6), the physical angular speed is

    dtheta/dt = K tau^(-1-h)[1+O(tau^beta)],
    K=d0^(1+h) F0(0,eta0)>0.

Because beta>h, the error is integrable up to time one. Therefore, after fixing the initial unwrapped angle,

    theta(t) = (K/h) tau^(-h) + Ctheta + O(tau^(beta-h)).       (7)

This proves a rotating positive-radius material-carrier existence statement for the specified completed construction, subject to its cited source theorems. It supplies neither an effective way to compute g nor a finite-volume information carrier. The tangential speed r*dtheta/dt has order tau^(gamma-1-h), which tends to zero under the above bounds, despite the divergent angular frequency. The total parcel speed nevertheless diverges axially: since eta0<0, its axial axis value is Uaxis=-D eta0/d0>0, and u_z=q^(-A)[Uaxis+o(1)] tends to positive infinity.

## Finite-horizon initialization and disturbances

Flatten the stable sheet by e=eta-eta0-g(rho,X). Its exact invariance and the C1 field give e'=b(rho,X,e)e, where b tends to a at the equilibrium. This follows by subtracting the e=0 equation and using a one-variable integral derivative; g need only be C1. On a sufficiently small compact neighborhood, a-epsilon<=b<=a+epsilon for any fixed small epsilon>0. If an added disturbance in this flattened equation has magnitude at most nu, then, as long as the perturbed path remains in that neighborhood,

    |e(s)| <= |e(0)| exp(Bs) + (nu/B)(exp(Bs)-1), B=a+epsilon.

In particular |e(0)|+nu/B <= m exp(-BS) suffices for |e(s)|<=m through horizon S, provided the other coordinates are also kept in the chosen neighborhood. This is a bound in normalized transverse error and rescaled time; arbitrary physical forcing errors are not automatically bounded by nu. With no disturbance and e(0) nonzero, the lower estimate |e(s)|>=|e(0)|exp((a-epsilon)s) quantifies the unstable direction while the path stays there.

Along the reference carrier, (5) gives S=(1/k)log(q0/qf)+O(1) as qf->0 for fixed q0. Equation (7) then gives S=(1/(kh))log M+O(1) for M accumulated angular ticks. Thus the sufficient normalized transverse tolerance scales, up to fixed factors, as

    M^(-B/(kh)).

For M=2^n this is exponentially small in n but its binary precision requirement is O(n) with parameters fixed. This description-length conclusion does not compute the center g(rho0,X0), realize the tolerance, bound controller work, or give a polynomial simulator. In particular there is no known polynomial modulus for constructing the source cutoff sequence or the stable graph in this analysis.

On the exact inner flow, two ideal labeled point tracers initialized with identical (r,z) and angular separation pi retain that separation, by axisymmetry. Their Euclidean separation is exactly 2r, of order tau^gamma, or M^(-gamma/h) at late tick budgets. This is a geometric separation calculation only. Diffusion, finite tracer size, readout outside the core, independent writable values, and interaction with the fluid remain unmodeled.
