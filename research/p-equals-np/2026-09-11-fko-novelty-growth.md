# Novelty audit: capped focused dependency growth

2026-09-11; S3068. Research comparison under [INTEGRITY-CLAIMS](../../INTEGRITY-CLAIMS.md), not a new proof review, priority certification or publication recommendation. Scope: [S3066 theorem](2026-09-11-focused-growth.md) and its [S3064 ancestry](2026-09-11-fko-discovery.md). Search cutoff September 11, 2026.

## Assessment

**Tentative classification: a possibly unrecorded policy-specific application of established probabilistic tools; novelty and publication significance remain unestablished.** No exact matching theorem was located in the focused search below. That is weaker than establishing a new result. The adaptive transcript argument is real mathematical work, especially after collisions, but its ingredients are deferred decisions, first-moment counting, conditional exponential moments and a union bound. No new general probabilistic method, improved FKO discovery algorithm or general local-algorithm lower bound follows.

The exact audited statement uses m=ceil(C n^(7/5)) independent uniform three-variable clause occurrences, K=ceil(A n^(1/5)), maximum-overlap growth with uniform ties, first unsigned dependence stopping, and fresh memoryless restarts on the unchanged input before the first success. For log R=o(n^(1/5) log n), no first dependence is obtained with high probability. Its exponential per-attempt term is restricted to good input events; the small-dependency and degree exceptions remain separate. It is not an unconditional exponential rarity statement, and runs sharing an input are not independent.

The coding interpretation is useful but insufficient for priority: with incidence matrix B of size n by m, a dependency is a nonzero word of ker B. Its column weight is three, while average check degree grows as n^.4 and the rate approaches one. A fixed-degree LDPC distance theorem cannot simply be applied uniformly as its check degree grows. The searches did not recover a primary theorem with the exact growing-degree ensemble and adaptive policy. This is an access/search limit, not an assertion that the coding literature lacks one.

## What could be specific to this application

The candidate contribution is the complete combination of: (i) a product-uniform completion invariant for the actual coarse transcript after collisions; (ii) counting first crossings into overlap at least two; (iii) the fact that an even subset of e selected clauses forces at least e/4 such selected crossings; (iv) a visible stopping rule that avoids conditioning on a global degree event; and (v) shared-input restart accounting that pays exceptional inputs only once. The first moment supplies e=Omega(n^.2), whereas the stopped crossing mean is O(n^(-.2)). A first-collision estimate alone would not support the stated restart range.

This is stronger than an observation that most prefixes initially resemble trees. It is still a specialized proof application, and this audit found no source demonstrating that the combination answers a previously identified open analytic question in its exact capped form. Calling it an immediate corollary of a named exploration theorem would also overstate what was verified: an explicit policy/transcript mapping remains necessary.

## Is the cap substantive?

It has an understandable motivation: FKO certificates contain tuples on the n^.2 scale. However, **short output is not short search prefix**. Wu's method can explore more clauses and discard them during pruning. The cap therefore excludes a potentially essential operation, not just an implementation constant. First unsigned dependence also stops before a later odd dependency could appear. Memoryless restart rules exclude learning from failed exploration.

Consequently the theorem supports rejecting this precise short-prefix strategy in our investigation. It does not support rejecting focused search, proving a barrier at the best known refutation exponent, or claiming that all polynomial-time adaptive algorithms fail. If A is below the global minimum-tuple constant, failure already follows from the first moment; the adaptive argument matters for caps beyond that trivial range. No new investigation is activated by this assessment.

The July 2026 results already checked in [S3065 sources](2026-09-11-adaptive-fko-sources.md) remain separate: hypergraph Moore existence (2607.14068v2, July 17) and sharp Kikuchi refutation (2607.29672v1, July 31) neither analyze this policy nor turn its failure into a general search lower bound. Both are preprints; prior access covered relevant statements/operators, not a full independent proof endorsement.
