# Existing tools for costs across representation changes

2026-09-11; S3063. Focused primary-source investigation under the [integrity boundary](../../INTEGRITY-CLAIMS.md), reusing the [S3062 atlas](2026-09-11-method-shapes.md). Three tools are evaluated below. No experiment, new lower-bound theorem, novelty claim or universal cost invariant is supplied.

**Finding:** a cost can survive a representation change when an explicit simulation, query-preservation or access-model contract carries it across. Semantic equivalence alone supplies none of these contracts. The strongest concrete composition obstacle found here is an existing compilation result; the most useful lower-bound transfer framework is charged proof simulation.

## 1. Proof simulation, lifting and discovery

**Primary theorem/definition.** Cook–Reckhow Definition 1.5 defines p-simulation by a polynomial-time map translating every proof while preserving the statement proved; Proposition 1.6 transfers polynomial boundedness in the simulation direction. This is a uniform proof translator, not a short-proof finder. [Original paper, Section 1](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf).

**A substantive existing transfer.** Goos–Koroth–Mertz–Pitassi Theorem 4 lifts resolution block-width to a CP length bound on a *gadget-composed* formula. With n blocks of ell variables and an appropriate gadget of size parameter m=(n ell)^Theta(1), it bounds CP length below by m^Omega(block-width(F)). The input is F composed with the specified gadget, not F under an arbitrary alternate notation. Their Theorem 1 constructs an always-UNSAT A(F): satisfiable F gives polynomial CP proofs, while unsatisfiable F gives length 2^(n^Omega(1)). This yields CP nonautomatability unless P=NP. The lower bound permits their semantic CP rules; the short-proof direction already uses resolution. Their Section 2 explains the communication/lifting connection. It does not establish that arbitrary PB extensions, EF, or mixed solvers admit the same translation. [Theorems 1 and 4, Sections 1.2–2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.08037).

**Actionable conditional contract [synthesis].** Fix a reference proof system P and an explicit admissible hybrid calculus H. Require a uniform compiler mapping every terminating H refutation of original F, including its encoding changes, definitions and imported lemmas, to a P refutation of F with length at most p(|F|+T), where T charges the complete hybrid computation and p is one fixed polynomial. A P length lower bound then constrains this H computation through that bound. The obligation is to construct the compiler, not merely name p or the total cost.

Interleaving is covered only if the compiler accepts the **whole mixed trace**. Translations of isolated endpoint proofs do not automatically handle lemmas learned in another representation, projection, extension variables, numerical certificates or rebuilding. Repeatedly composing polynomial bounds can itself produce an exponent that grows with the number of switches; a direct global bound is required. A size simulation alone can transfer proof-size facts, but efficient discovery comparisons additionally require uniform transformations and compatible shortest-proof bounds in the needed direction.

**Why the known escapes matter.** A polynomial translation of arbitrary Gaussian refutations of standard expander-Tseitin CNFs into resolution would contradict their known resolution size bounds. Thus that proposed reference system fails to cover the escape. Strengthening P may accommodate Gaussian or counting but lose the available lower bound. Also, the always-UNSAT image A(F) in the automatability theorem illustrates why difficulty of producing a specified proof is not the same as deciding a promised instance's truth value.

## 2. Exact compilation: succinctness, query and join costs

**Primary result.** Darwiche–Marquis separate succinctness (Definition 3.1, existence of polynomial-size equivalent representations) from supported polynomial-time queries and transformations (Propositions 4.1 and 5.1). Their Appendix proof of Proposition 5.1, printed pp.259–260, uses NP-completeness of consistency of the conjunction of two OBDDs with different variable orders. Consequently arbitrary binary conjunction cannot be computed in polynomial time into DNNF, d-DNNF, FBDD or OBDD unless P=NP, since those outputs support polynomial-time consistency. Binary conjunction for a common fixed OBDD order is supported instead. [Compilation map, definitions, tables and Appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1819).

**Reduction check and attribution.** The Appendix cites Meinel?Theobald Lemma 8.14; its original book proof was not accessed. The following independently checked reconstruction makes the existing hardness claim transparent. Replace each literal occurrence of a 3-CNF by a fresh Boolean variable. One OBDD reads occurrence variables clause by clause and accepts exactly when every signed clause is satisfied. A second reads them grouped by original variable and accepts exactly when all occurrences in each group agree. Both scans need only constant state per position and linear size; their conjunction is consistent exactly when the original formula is satisfiable. Evaluating both diagrams on a proposed assignment is polynomial, giving membership in NP. The orders differ over the same variable universe. This is hardness of the joint **decision query**, not just an output-size assertion; it does not say every mismatched pair is hard. This reconstruction checks a known result and is not proposed as a new lemma.

**What survives [synthesis].** A representation supporting an easy individual query can move the hard work into conversion or combination. The two-OBDD result is a precise existing example of that phenomenon, not a universal conservation law. Maintaining a common order avoids this particular conditional obstruction but does not bound intermediate sizes after an input-length-dependent sequence of joins. Allowing a compact raw CNF conjunction preserves exact semantics cheaply while giving up the target's cheap consistency query. A small equivalent object existing does not provide its construction algorithm.

This is an honest candidate comparison tool: specify the required joint query and the permitted order/decomposition interfaces before asserting that two tractable representations combine tractably. It does not make ordinary XOR systems hard, prove every conversion expensive, or justify a new demonstration of already known join behavior.

## 3. Quantum adversary/state-conversion bounds

**Primary result.** Lee–Mittal–Reichardt–Spalek–Szegedy Theorem 1.1 characterizes bounded-error quantum query complexity of finite-domain functions by the general adversary bound, up to constants. Their state-conversion framework uses initial/target Gram matrices and filters determined by input queries. Theorem 4.9 has error-parameter slack; the following discussion explicitly warns that arbitrary approximate state-conversion problems need not have a matching-error characterization without a robustness condition. Coherent and noncoherent output requirements also differ. [Definitions 2.1–2.2, Theorems 1.1 and 4.9](https://www.ucw.cz/~robert/papers/state-conversion.pdf).

**Useful scope.** Under the same oracle and output requirement, arbitrary input-independent transformations between queries do not evade a valid query lower bound. Input-dependent preprocessing and state preparation must either be included in the algorithm's queries or represented as supplied initial information with its cost charged separately. A bound starting from an already informative input-dependent state is not a lower bound on preparing that state for free. The theorem does not bound circuit synthesis, memory access or physical execution costs.

**Decisive explicit-input limit [elementary scope check].** For a fixed-length explicit CNF encoding of L bits, a query algorithm can read all L bits and then use arbitrarily expensive free computation to decide SAT. Thus its input-bit query complexity is at most L. This model cannot establish a superpolynomial time lower bound in L. By contrast, treating all 2^n candidate assignments as black-box oracle entries hides the explicit clause structure. Lower bounds in that assignment-oracle model cannot be transferred to an algorithm allowed to inspect and preprocess the CNF.

The honest use is comparing prescribed oracle subroutines or state conversions with the same access, promise, error and preparation contract. It is not a quantum-inclusive invariant for arbitrary explicit SAT algorithms. The broader quantum resource questions in the [S3061 alternatives note](2026-09-11-solver-frontier-alternatives.md) remain separate; deferred amplification work is not reopened.

## Candidate disposition

The meaningful next mathematical obligation, if a hybrid method is selected, is an actual uniform translation of its complete allowed derivations into a reference system with an applicable same-family bound, or an explicit counterexample to that translation. No such reference system and compiler covering all our classical and quantum alternatives has been established. Merely minimizing over representations or naming total running time would restate the original complexity problem.

The compilation join result already supplies a substantive restricted answer to “what remains costly”: coordinating independently tractable representations can be computationally hard under a specified query contract. It is an established result, not a new research contribution. A focused current check of [Capelli?s August 2026 compilation lecture](https://florent.capelli.me/talks/260828-rw/) confirms the common-order product construction and practical order-selection difficulty; it did not identify a stated new shared-order discovery frontier suitable for this task. No such new target is claimed from absence of a search result, and unrelated open compilation questions are not imported. No credible universal transfer, novel invariant, experiment or proof-search campaign follows automatically from these sources.
