# S3052: restricted simulation bridge and nonvacuity assessment

2026-09-11. S3052 / E014 / E004, under INTEGRITY-CLAIMS.md. **STOP the proposed transfer from LOWESA to a deterministic polynomial general-SAT decision algorithm.** The selected theorem has a different angle/error guarantee, and the current QAOA family has no uniform SAT decision gap. The elementary examples below clarify those failures but do not identify a new theorem or publishable obstruction. No successor, simulation or proof campaign is recommended by this gate.

## Exact target being tested

Input is a succinct CNF F of bit length N on n variables, clause width at most a fixed k<=8 and at most a fixed number Delta of clause occurrences per variable. This is a theoretical bounded-degree family, not the previously sampled dense random-8SAT ensemble. Let q denote clause count and d denote QAOA depth. The circuit starts in |+>^n and alternates the violation-count phase exp(-i gamma_j H_F/2) and mixer exp(-i beta_j sum X/2). Each layer shares its beta and gamma across the corresponding gates; all angles are fixed by a uniformly supplied polynomial-size description, not independently resampled per gate. A depth may be fixed or grow polynomially with N, but any claimed algorithm must state which regime it handles.

For the strongest direct model comparison, expand each constant-width cost phase into Pauli-Z rotations, retaining shared-angle relations and clause multiplicity, with polynomially many rotations m=O(d(n+q*2^k)). Put an independent single-qubit Pauli channel after each Pauli rotation on its support, with pX=pY=pZ=eta and fixed 0<eta<1/3; other Clifford gates are noiseless. This deliberately matches the selected noise placement. It is a theoretical local-noise model, not a measured hardware claim or an assertion that earlier ideal simulations used noise. General dephasing-only noise, angle-dependent errors and error-corrected logical circuits are different cases requiring a fresh match.

The desired quantity is a_F=Tr(Pi_F rho_F), where Pi_F=sum over satisfying x of |x><x| and rho_F is the noisy output. The input describes Pi_F by F and an efficient classical assignment verifier. It does not provide an exponentially long Pauli expansion or free solutions/counts. The target task is a numerical estimate with enough accuracy to distinguish the intended decision cases, not a local energy estimate, full-state output or generic weak sampling task.

There is no established lower bound a_F>=1/poly(N) on every satisfiable input for these circuits; UNSAT gives a_F=0. Consequently, additive1/12 is not automatically meaningful for SAT. It would be sufficient for the different hypothetical bounded-error decider in the [S3051 bridge contract](2026-09-11-complexity-implication-map.md), whose acceptance is >=2/3 versus <=1/3. That decider premise has not been proved for current QAOA.

## Published theorem and hypothesis match

Selected source: Fontana et al., *Classical simulations of noisy variational quantum circuits*, npj Quantum Information 11, 84 (published 2025), [published PDF](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/92079/7/Fontana-etal-QI-2025-Classical-simulations-of-noisy-variational-quantum-circuits.pdf). Theorem 1/Eq. 19 gives deterministic LOWESA with full-independent-angle-cube RMSE bounded by (1-2 p_noise)^(ell+1) <= exp(-2 p_noise*ell), and time O(n^2 m 2^ell), where p_noise=min(pX,pY,pZ)>0. Corollary2 randomizes angles, not simulator coins. Theorem3 handles a Pauli expansion with a coefficient quasi-norm; Theorem6 already discusses correlated-parameter limitations. See the [full source contract](2026-09-11-restricted-simulation-source-contract.md) for exact theorem text, assumptions and version/access evidence.

## Elementary nonvacuity and representation checks

**1. Constant additive accuracy can reveal no SAT information.** Take F_n=x_1 AND ... AND x_n, with each variable occurring once. It has exactly one satisfying assignment. Use any positive depth with all QAOA angles zero and retain the specified local noise channels. Initial computational-basis outcomes are uniform, and mixtures of local Pauli errors preserve that uniform diagonal distribution. Thus a_F=2^(-n). For n>=4, the constant-zero estimator has error at most1/12. It also gives the exact answer0 for every UNSAT projector, so meeting this tolerance alone cannot distinguish these two decision cases.

This is not a hard SAT family: conjunctions are easy to solve classically. Its role is to falsify the inference 'a constant-additive success estimate automatically decides SAT,' even inside a bounded-degree, fixed-angle family. It is not a new result or a counterexample to LOWESA. It does not assert that the previously published nonzero angle arrays produce this state.

For uniform preparation on arbitrary formulas, a_F=K/2^n. Distinguishing K=0 from K>=1 by a generic additive estimator can use epsilon<=2^(-n)/3 and threshold2^(-n)/2. Substituting that epsilon into a polynomial-in1/epsilon runtime guarantee does not certify polynomial complexity in n. A relative-error guarantee below one on positive probabilities, with exact handling of zero, would also resolve the positivity problem; no such general efficient guarantee follows here. Random uniform sampling of assignments already supplies weak samples efficiently, while detecting a unique satisfying sample has exponentially small per-trial probability. Weak sampling is not exact strong simulation or an exact solution counter.

**2. Succinct verification is not the same input interface as a Pauli list.** For the same conjunction, Pi_F=product_i(I-Z_i)/2 has 2^n Pauli terms of magnitude2^(-n). Naively materializing the expansion is exponential. However, its coefficient l1 norm is1 and sampling a random subset/sign is easy, so term count alone does not prove every observable-estimation approach inefficient.

For the specific deterministic Theorem3 bound, r=ln2/(ln2+2p_noise)<1 and the cost factor is (||c||_r/epsilon)^(ln2/(2p_noise)). With all2^n terms included, ||c||_r=2^(2p_noise*n/ln2), giving a2^n factor even at constant epsilon. Removing the exactly handled identity term preserves this asymptotic factor for fixed noise. This substitution describes that published bound, not an impossibility theorem or a refutation of potential randomized coefficient sampling. Such an alternative would need its own variance, input-access, fixed-angle and error proof.

A polynomial-size coherent verifier could turn a global SAT test into an output-qubit measurement, but appending that verifier changes the circuit and its fixed gates/angles and noise. It does not automatically restore the independent-angle premise or give the original noiseless verification probability. This alternative must be analyzed under a theorem that actually covers the appended circuit, not assumed free.

**3. Clause expectations do not determine satisfying probability.** For F=x_1 AND x_2, compare the diagonal state assigning probability1/2 to each of00 and11 with the state assigning probability1/2 to each of01 and10. Each clause has violation expectation1/2 in both states; total expected violation count is1 in both. The satisfying probability is1/2 in the first state and0 in the second. This is an elementary moment example, not an assertion that both states arise from the same fixed noisy QAOA circuit or an attack on a theorem that estimates global observables. It shows why replacing Pi_F with H_F loses the requested information without extra distributional assumptions.

## Decision and stopping boundary

**STOP this theorem-to-general-SAT transfer.** The published result does not supply pointwise correlated-angle accuracy, polynomial access/accuracy for the intended global test, or the missing all-input quantum SAT decision gap. The correlation issue is already recognized in the source; the small-probability and local-moment examples are routine checks. No genuinely new missing lemma or counterexample target was identified at this gate, so there is no GO for a new proof or simulator project.

A theorem giving a nonvacuous, uniform correlated-angle guarantee for a useful restricted family could still be valuable, but no such new target has been specified beyond existing work here. Postulating a general quantum SAT decider plus a sufficiently accurate deterministic simulator would merely restate the two unresolved premises in the implication map. Neither their conjunction nor an intermediate complexity-class separation has been established.

Earlier outcomes remain unchanged: [S3048](2026-09-11-qaoa-logical-cost-report.md) stops one construction's unitary-gate advantage on seven tested formulas; [S3050](2026-09-11-qaoa-contribution-assessment.md) holds the combined publication candidate. This gate reused those boundaries and literature; it performed no benchmark, numerical simulation, proof campaign, source outreach, hardware work or publication. It establishes no P=NP/P!=NP result and no general classical-simulation impossibility.
