# Golden-collision transfer: independent source and complexity review

## Source requirement independently reconstructed

[Jaques-Schrottenloher, SAC 2020 official preproceedings](https://sacworkshop.org/SAC20/files/preproceedings/04-QuantumSearch.pdf), Problem 2.1 and Appendix B, assumes a random self-map with a designated golden collision. Lemma B.2 conditions on the two endpoints having equal images and supplies their joint predecessor tail. Theorem B.2 also needs enough predecessors reachable within the iteration cutoff; large basin size alone is insufficient. Theorem B.3 gives probability Omega(1/t) of a marked-subset fraction Omega(R^2 min(u^4,t^2)/N^2), for t=O(1/theta), in the preceding algorithm's parameter regime. Reaching a distinguished point after merging is separately charged. Neither a mean predecessor count nor existence of some collision is this statement. These locations were reread directly for this checkpoint; no claim of auditing the entire published proof is made.

## Closest nonuniform-map tools

[Aldous-Miermont-Pitman, math/0401115v1, 12 January 2004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401115), equation (1), defines a p-mapping by independent destinations drawn from p. The introduction and Theorem 1 framework concern scaling limits of exploration and basin/cyclic structure, with explicit assumptions on p and its squared-mass norm. These are not finite-size joint tails for two specified original indices conditioned to form a golden collision. A weak convergence result cannot silently supply the polynomially small success probability required here.

For a fixed key partition F, the class transition z -> F(R(z)) has precisely independent destination probabilities |F^-1(z')|/N when each R(z) is an independent uniform original index. This elementary model identification was sent as consultation, not claimed as a novel theorem. It locates a useful prior framework, but erases which particular member of a fiber was selected. Golden endpoint predecessors and original witness extraction need that information. No class-level basin theorem is promoted to an index-level algorithm bound.

[Hansen-Jaworski, math/0603529v1, 22 March 2006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0603529), defines another model using exchangeable indegrees and equal probabilities for mappings with a fixed indegree sequence. Its definition and conditional-uniformity use were inspected. A fixed partition constraining whole groups of source indices to share an image imposes more than a target indegree sequence. Thus matching indegree marginals does not establish this model's conditional law. Its global component conclusions cannot be imported merely because target labels are exchangeable. Related predecessor-paper abstracts were located but no unaccessed theorem is relied on.

## Necessary distinctions for the proposed transfer

Index-level direct arrivals into two fixed endpoints, the mass of their entire truncated predecessor sets, and aggregate success over many useful claws are different quantities. A bound for one fixed pair cannot establish failure for a decoder with many acceptable pairs. Conversely, many repeated encodings do not by themselves prove the random-map predecessor tail.

Canonicalization, unique invalid labels and restrictions must preserve a useful minimum-price comparator with charged success probability. They must also support polynomial-bit evaluation of the randomized map; an ideal fully random function on exponentially many keys is not a free circuit. Any proposed seed family needs the independence or pseudorandomness actually used by its proof. Random-FKO statements require an explicit conditioned law for every relevant price query, or an appropriately uniform success statement.

Searches on 2026-09-11 included `random mappings prescribed indegree sequence predecessor distribution p mappings` and `random mappings nonuniform distribution basin sizes prescribed indegrees Hansen Jaworski`. Primary PDFs and indicated sections above were opened. No exhaustive classification or novelty claim follows. No code, experiments, commits or publication were performed.

## Prefix alternative independently checked

The same SAC source, Section 3.5 and equations (10)-(11), uses a prefix bucket rather than predecessor iteration. This is an essential alternative, not ruled out by the earlier mismatch. Its cost needs efficient uniform bucket preparation as well as a useful bucket-size bound. An upper expectation alone does not guarantee the claimed preparation density. A fixed useful claw suffices to lower-bound marked subsets if the colored counter is implemented correctly; many useful claws need not satisfy an artificial uniqueness requirement. The author is examining the required size and sampling conditions. No full transfer is approved before that argument is saved and checked.

[Yoder-Low-Chuang, 1409.3305v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3305), equations (1)-(5), was independently opened. Its fixed-point sequence prepares the normalized target component from the initial state with small failure amplitude using a known lower overlap bound. Because the initial distribution is uniform and the predicate treats every bucket member identically, that target is the uniform bucket state. A bucket-dependent phase is harmless: absorb it into the ideal preparation isometry; it cancels in the coin projector and V*SV. A state error eta gives O(eta) projector/reflection error, uniformly over bucket labels, which supports a hybrid bound through the walk and outer amplification. This is not an operator-norm approximation of an arbitrary full preparation unitary on arbitrary inputs. Finite gate synthesis of controlled phases to the allocated error costs polynomial logarithmic factors, and must remain included in those factors.

## Significance and disposition

The known SAC Section 3.5 architecture and N^(6/7) exponent are the direct ancestry. The light/heavy split and dummy floor remove the specific arbitrary-fiber obstacle for this reviewed construction. This is a candidate extension to the route's worst-case weighted key domain, not a verified publication-priority claim. The earlier S3075 no-transfer assessment is superseded locally by this new derivation; it was not a universal lower bound.

The active-gate count is an ordinary bounded-fan-in circuit bound with idle storage uncharged, and space remains N^(2/7) times polynomial factors. This establishes neither practical quantum advantage nor polynomial-time SAT, a fastest random-FKO refuter, or a resolution of P versus NP.
