## Claims checked

- **Established core and local exploration.** The rank formula and minimal affine state interface are attributed to existing coding/matroid theory. Conditioning the star, Gaussian construction, equality substitution, and recognition of the adjacent XOR relation are standard or elementary operations. Neither the source note nor the author calls the local reconstruction a newly discovered interface theorem. The paired-path interaction is a locally derived test with unestablished historical novelty, not a publication claim.
- **Exact decision mechanism.** This attempt streams states and stops at the first witness. It does not require a full model count or survivor cover. Its soundness and completeness are separated from its cost; the finite fallback in local solvers is explicitly exponential in the free leaf dimension. A supplied separator and partition are not a free optimal-decomposition oracle.
- **Fixed cut versus arbitrary algorithms.** The pure affine minimum of 2^t rectangles is confined to the specified partition and requires feasibility. The filtered relation instead has one rectangle per jointly surviving syndrome. These representation facts are explicitly distinguished from decision runtime. Row operations and within-block changes preserve the stated interface, while arbitrary mixing across blocks or selecting a different partition falls outside the invariance claim.
- **Fixed ordering versus all search policies.** The paired-path lower bound is exactly floor(2^k/3)+1 attempts for zero-offset, ascending standard-basis enumeration. It does not claim failure of every ordering, preprocessing rule, compatibility algorithm, or quantum method. Its exponentially many attempts concern this operation; the explicit formula has a polynomial repair and is not presented as a hard SAT family.
- **Small output versus finding it.** Exactly two surviving syndromes do not bound the effort of the stated unfiltered search. The author derives both the two survivors and the first survivor's position, without treating a semantic filter as free. The substitution repair actually constructs the XOR chain with charged sparse representation work. That repair is asserted only for this explicit family; no general recognition, compatible-cut, or growth theorem is inferred.
- **Time, storage, and encoding.** Interface construction uses polynomial linear algebra, but the counts of separator assignments, streamed syndromes, and actual local checks remain charged. Streaming does not imply an exponential-memory lower bound. Sparse indexed input size is distinguished from the number of variables; the attempt count is exponential in k and superpolynomial in the stated bit encoding, without asserting exponential dependence on encoded input length.
- **General derivations versus finite checks.** Five controls and 35 family cases support implementation fidelity. The all-k rank, fiber, and alternating-string statements have explicit algebraic derivations. Subsecond local timing is expressly not a runtime study. Independent review checks are separately reported as finite verification, not as proof by testing or human peer review.
- **Connection to the broader objective.** The attempt tests a concrete computational change and refutes its proposed survivor-count efficiency criterion. It establishes neither P=NP nor P!=NP, a general SAT lower bound, a uniform efficient SAT solver, a new quantum advantage, nor a general repair for arbitrary mixed systems. The earlier ordinary-resolution and materialized-representation obstructions retain their scoped meaning.
