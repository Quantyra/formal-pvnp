## Wording versus theorem content

- `marginal` is the actual finite sum of prior times kernel and `posterior` divides that joint mass by the marginal. `marginal_normalized` assumes normalized prior and kernel rows. `posterior_normalized` requires strictly positive marginal; it is not a probability-distribution assertion at a zero marginal.
- Zero-prior atoms remain in the carrier and receive zero posterior mass. `bayes_ratio` requires positive prior, but not positive marginal: the zero-marginal case uses rational division by zero. This is an algebraic identity, not a conditional-probability interpretation of a null observation. `joint_zero_of_marginal_zero` uses nonnegative joint summands; `total_probability` uses this to include null observations safely. The receipt accurately distinguishes these cases.
- `posterior_event_cutoff` assumes nonnegative prior/kernel, nonnegative C, and a pointwise posterior bound on the specified good set. Its conclusion is event mass at most C times that event's prior mass plus posterior mass of the bad set. It does not multiply all prior mass by C or establish the pointwise likelihood premise. Its algebra also covers zero marginal, where the posterior is the zero vector rather than a normalized law.
- `normalized_reweighting` explicitly assumes a nonnegative normalized rational law, weights in [0,1], 0 < p0 <= 1, nonnegative eta and zeta, good-set relative stability, bad mass <= zeta, and eta + zeta/p0 <= 1/2. It derives Z >= p0/2 > 0, reweighted bad mass <= 2*zeta/p0, and the absolute expectation difference <= 4*eta + 4*zeta/p0 for every rational response in [0,1]. `reweighted_normalized` supplies the normalization identity under positive Z. The author wording matches these statements.
- Checks inspect zero-prior, zero-marginal and zero-weight examples, including a satisfiable nontrivial 7/8 versus 1/8 reweighting example. These examples establish neither universal applicability of the stability assumptions nor geometry-specific estimates.
