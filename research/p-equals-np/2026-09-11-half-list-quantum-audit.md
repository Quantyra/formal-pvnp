## Primary contracts

[Tani, arXiv:0708.2584v2, pp. 1-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.2584) explicitly extends claw finding to a fixed relation R on p+q queried values. For p=q=1 and equally sized domains N, the stated bound is O(N^(2/3)) queries. Therefore a weighted syndrome/sign relation is not excluded simply because it is more general than equality. The theorem measures oracle queries, not the gate cost of evaluating a marked subset from its retained records. Its search procedure also recovers a pair; it is not merely a trace/detection observable.

[Magniez-Nayak-Roland-Santha, arXiv:quant-ph/0608026, Section 1.3 and Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0608026) charges quantum setup S, reversible update U (including inverses), and marked-state phase checking C. Its bound is S+(1/sqrt(epsilon))[(1/sqrt(delta))U+C], with stationary marked probability at least epsilon and chain gap delta. Data associated with a walk state must support the specified coherent transformations. An arbitrary marked predicate is allowed, but its computational implementation is not free.

[Ambainis, arXiv:quant-ph/0311001v9, Section 6, printed pp. 24-25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0311001) explicitly implements fast data operations in the circuit model augmented by random-access quantum-memory gates. It requires a representation independent of insertion history and bounded-time subroutines. Its stated deletion-cost obstruction without those gates concerns that implementation, not a universal quantum lower bound. The source combines hashing and skip lists for its fast memory operations. Thus importing its query exponent as a count of ordinary one- and two-qubit gates would change the access model without justification.

## What the cost formula does and does not buy

For two N-element index domains, take a product of Johnson walks retaining s indices from each domain. One fixed successful cross-pair is included with probability (s/N)^2. The chain gap has order 1/s away from the trivial endpoints. If preparation costs s times a polynomial in the original input, and coherent update/check costs are polynomial in that input and log N, the formula yields approximately s+N/sqrt(s), optimized at s=N^(2/3). Those are conditional substitutions, not a proof of the update data structure or its physical memory model.

A scan of all s retained records in every update instead contributes approximately N sqrt(s), removing that particular sublinear-time conclusion. A quantum scan of s^2 pairs at every marked test also cannot simply be called constant checking cost. Neither diagnostic is an all-algorithm obstruction. The actual state preparation, memory organization, reversible scratch cleanup and update/check implementations decide whether the favorable formula applies.

For a weighted bucket implementation, retaining only its current cheapest item is insufficient for deletion of that item. One needs the next cheapest retained item, tie-breaking by encoding ID, and coherent restoration on inverse updates. Ordinary mutable dictionaries can represent the same logical set differently after different histories; that extra information can prevent interference. A canonical representation, or an explicitly justified equivalent quantum data structure, is required. All bit lengths include the rational multiplicative-weights prices from S3073.

## Candidate escape: reduce the weighted threshold to equality

The following elementary reduction is submitted for independent checking; this reviewer originated it and should not count as its independent approver.

Fix a queried nonnegative price vector after conversion to common-denominator integers. Let a and b be the two half prices, and let T be an integer threshold. Choose B so that every half price is in [0,2^B-1]. For each valid left half, put z=min(T-a,2^B-1). If z<0, emit no valid label. Otherwise partition [0,z] into its canonical aligned dyadic intervals. There are at most B+1 such intervals. Label each by the pair (prefix length, prefix bits). For each valid right half of price b, emit all B+1 prefixes of its B-bit encoding, including the empty prefix. Equality of an interval label and a right prefix holds exactly when b belongs to that interval. Hence a left/right label match exists exactly when a+b<=T.

Append the exact incidence syndrome and a cardinality pair (i,j) to the labels. Left records require sign zero and size i; right records require sign one and size j; only i+j<=k is admissible. Extra partner-cardinality and interval/prefix indices increase the two domain sizes by a polynomial in k and B. Missing slots and wrong-sign records receive invalid labels with disjoint left/right tags, so they cannot match. Valid output labels use a separate tag. A real pair retains the underlying encoding IDs for exact recovery.

This is a two-function claw reduction. Equal labels within one side, including duplicate encodings of the same half, must not be mistaken for a successful cross-pair. At a valid cross-match, opposite signs ensure the symmetric difference is nonempty. Recovering it and verifying its incidence/sign/support and actual price remain polynomial operations on the original IDs.

The reduction replaces the bespoke dynamic weighted-minimum marked predicate by the ordinary equality-claw predicate with polynomial index expansion. It does not remove the quantum-memory access cost of a fast claw implementation, and it does not prove the original list's index decoder. An integer threshold binary search uses only polynomially many threshold bits, rather than one step per price value. If used inside adaptive multiplicative weights, its bounded-error calls require an explicit error budget across all threshold searches and packing rounds. Exact final certificate verification prevents a failed search from creating a false refutation; claimed mass performance still depends on successful searches.

Read the saved [half-list-quantum.md](2026-09-11-half-list-quantum.md), including its b>=1 convention and explicitly tagged invalid values. The forest domain correctly charges root fields, neighbor slots and malformed encodings; rounding to binary fields adds only its stated exponential-in-h and polynomial factors. This is an explicit decoder, rather than a claim that a sequential enumerator automatically has random access. Duplicate forest encodings do not invalidate coverage or the cross-claw contract.

Threshold binary search and extraction use polynomially many price bits. Their success budget across the polynomial number of adaptive packing rounds is sound: condition on each classical prior history, apply the same worst-case search guarantee, then union bound. Final tuple, packing and spectral checks protect pointwise soundness even when an optimality search fails. I do not count this as independent approval of the dyadic reduction, which I also originated; the separate complexity reviewer checks it.

For the combined-domain Johnson walk, the colored adjacency test is correct. Sorting by (key,side,index) places the two side groups consecutively within each common key; an opposite-side equal-key adjacent pair exists exactly when that key has both colors. Inserting or deleting a record alters only constantly many adjacencies. The counter is a function of the current set, independent of the operation history. A fixed successful pair is included with probability of order (r_w/N')^2. The gap and setup/update substitutions yield the stated conditional N'^(2/3) scale. Scanning a returned marked subset costs r_w times record factors and is correctly charged. Replacing each addressed operation by a linear scan gives the displayed N'^(4/3) conservative gate upper bound at that choice of r_w; it is not a lower bound or proof of optimal ordinary-gate cost.

A source-level gap was identified and repaired: Ambainis's Section 6.2, Lemma 6, controls its fixed hash-bucket overflow using the one-collision walk's special amplitude symmetry. The generic colored marked predicate need not retain that symmetry, so local counter correctness alone does not import the entire original error analysis. The final author now uses [Akmal-Jin, Near-Optimal Quantum Algorithms for String Problems, Lemmas 3.13-3.14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-022-01092-x): limited-independence hashing supplies a uniform fixed-subset failure bound, and its dynamic ordered structures are history independent. Section 2.3 explicitly assumes quantum random-access gates.

The saved repair was independently checked. For each fixed logical operation input, the bad-seed probability is at most p_bad. Ideal amplitudes are seed independent, so averaging the squared bad-component norm over seeds preserves that bound for a superposition of logical inputs. Jensen's inequality and a hybrid comparison give expected state-vector error at most 2T sqrt(p_bad) across T operation boundaries. The chosen p_bad<=(rho/(8T))^2 leaves ample measurement-error allowance. This compares the implemented evolution to ideal seed-independent amplitudes; it never assumes independence for an already faulty trajectory, or one seed good on every subset. Fixed cutoffs, coherent error branches, inverse operations, enlarged seed parameters and later adaptive-call failures are accounted for. These checks support the augmented-memory time statement only.
