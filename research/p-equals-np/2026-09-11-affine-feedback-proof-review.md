## Soundness, exact local scope and termination

The [checker](2026-09-11-affine-feedback-mechanism.py) preserves the original affine-disjunctive clauses. Given the current consistent affine equations, its RREF parametrization `x=a+Bz` has free original coordinates equal to the corresponding z coordinates. A literal `u.x+c` becomes `u.Bz+(u.a+c)`. The code retains that offset when forming the literal-falsity equation. Canonical row reduction of a clause's full falsifying system is equivalent to the original disjunction: an inconsistent falsifying system is a tautological clause, and a zero-equation falsifying system makes the clause identically false.

The vectors used for scope enumeration are the rows of these **canonical clause-falsity systems**, not necessarily the original syntactically occurring literal vectors. This distinction was raised during review and must be retained in the operation's definition. Coordinates remain explicitly fixed; a change in the enumerated generator list is not by itself evidence that the resulting consequence closure changes.

For a queried span with independent basis vectors v_1,...,v_q, put `y_j=v_j.z`. The map from z onto these q coordinates is surjective. Every clause whose entire falsity-row space lies in the span can be evaluated exactly on y. Let T be the conjunction's allowed y values. If T is empty, the full problem is inconsistent. Otherwise all equations of its affine hull are sound consequences. The algorithm adds those equations while **retaining the clauses**. It does not replace a non-affine relation by its larger affine hull.

An equation `sum_j e_j y_j=f` becomes `(sum_j e_j v_j).z=f`. Because z consists of original free coordinates, lifting that mask to those original columns is exact without an additional offset. This simple lifting would require adjustment for a different parametrization whose free coordinates had nonzero offsets; the implemented RREF convention meets the needed condition.

Every nonzero learned local equation has a nonzero coefficient in the free quotient, since the queried basis is independent. It therefore increases the global affine rank unless the batch is inconsistent. At most n productive consistent passes occur, followed by a terminal pass. A fixed point means no new affine equations from the rule's current windows; `OPEN` is not a satisfiability verdict.

There is a useful precise completeness statement within this scope. Any collection of current clauses whose combined falsity-row span has rank at most w has a basis of at most w occurring rows. Its span is therefore enumerated. The algorithm collects at least those clauses there; adding other wholly contained clauses cannot invalidate their affine consequences. Conversely, every queried group itself has combined rank at most w. More exactly, replace a queried span V by the joint row span T of its collected clauses. T is also enumerated, and the collected clause sets are identical because T is contained in V. The V truth relation is the T relation with free extra coordinates, so its affine hull supplies no further constraints in those extra coordinates. Consequently the meaningful per-pass output is exactly the affine consequences generated by all bounded-joint-rank clause collections, independent of the chosen within-clause row bases, although candidate lists can differ. This does not cover arbitrary high-rank collections, variable projections of clauses outside the span, resolution, or general local consistency algorithms.

For fixed w, there are at most `(M+1)^w` candidate spans from M occurring rows, each with at most `2^w` local assignments. Hull extraction can use `4^w` times polynomial work. With the rank-progress bound, scanning all retained clauses, elimination, coordinate conversion, deduplication and dense coefficient storage can all be charged polynomially in explicit input size for fixed w. Clause count never grows through distributive expansion; denser affine coefficients still require polynomial storage. This is not polynomial dependence on a growing w.

## The strict window hierarchy

Let F_ell, ell>=3, have two implication cycles sharing x. The first is `x -> a_1 -> ... -> a_(ell-1) -> NOT x`; the second is `NOT x -> b_1 -> ... -> b_(ell-1) -> x`. It has `2ell-1` variables and `2ell` binary clauses. The first cycle forces x=0 and the second x=1, so F_ell is UNSAT.

Initially there are no affine equations, and each clause's canonical falsity rows are coordinate vectors. Every enumerated rank-at-most-w span is consequently a coordinate window. If w<ell, its induced clause graph contains neither complete cycle, hence is a forest. Each edge forbids exactly one of its four endpoint assignments.

Every such forest relation has full affine hull. To see this, fix a vertex v. Assign each neighbor the value that satisfies its incident clause regardless of v. Deleting v separates these neighbors into different tree components. Extend each chosen neighbor assignment down its component, choosing a child value satisfying its one parent edge; at most one child value is forbidden. The resulting assignment permits both values of v without changing other coordinates. Thus the relation has a pair of models differing by each coordinate unit vector. All unit vectors belong to the difference span, proving full affine hull. Isolated vertices satisfy the same claim immediately.

Therefore every window with w<ell is nonempty and supplies no nontrivial affine equation. The rule remains at rank zero and returns OPEN on this UNSAT formula. At w=ell, the full first-cycle window supplies x=0 and the full second-cycle window x=1; batch elimination detects inconsistency. For every fixed w there are arbitrarily large stalled F_ell with ell>w; ell=w+1 gives the adjacent strict hierarchy (w>=2).

This is not a general bounded-width or SAT lower bound. The family is an easy 2-SAT family, with binary clauses and a cactus primal graph. The failure concerns only the selected affine-hull feedback rule. Its input length is `O(ell log(ell+1))` with indexed variables; the hierarchy does not assume an enormous hidden encoding.
