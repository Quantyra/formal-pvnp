## Complexity obligations checked

1. **Extraction is charged and restricted.** The executable partitions normalized clauses by exact support and tests only buckets of at most three variables. It does not recognize arbitrary hidden parity. It enumerates local satisfying assignments and all candidate linear normals; the implementation therefore includes a `4^b poly(b)` recognition term, in addition to evaluating the bucket clauses on `2^b` assignments. Fixed b makes this polynomial. Enumerating arbitrary groups or supports would be an additional algorithm and cost. Exact equality with the affine relation is essential; replacing a relation by its affine hull is unsound.
2. **Quotient cost is explicit.** Gaussian elimination and the particular solution/basis construction are polynomial in the explicitly represented matrix size. Integer masks have dimension-dependent length. The executable retains all free coordinates, so its count has no omitted projection factor. An optional projection can preserve SAT only when its discarded directions are invisible to every residual constraint; counting also requires the constant fiber multiplicity.
3. **Every intermediate dictionary matters.** Let W_t be the number of nonzero keys after t events. Processing the next event visits every old key, performs an intersection reduction, copies the old dictionary, and merges coefficients. Simultaneous old/new storage and transient keys must be counted. The completed-stage peak is reported separately from the transient-new-dictionary peak; within an update the new dictionary has at most 2 W_t keys. Each affine key needs at most O(d^2) bits. Each coefficient is a signed sum of at most 2^t subset contributions and needs O(t+1) bits. Shifted count terms and accumulation use O(d+t+1) bits. A polynomial bound on every W_t would suffice for polynomial total bit work under a polynomial-cost dictionary implementation; a small final W_m alone would not.
4. **The star is a fixed-rule obstruction.** After any ordering of t distinct star clauses there are exactly 2^t surviving keys, with coefficients plus or minus one. Thus neither coefficient growth nor numerical error causes this failure. The family has m clauses and m+1 variables, with indexed binary input size O(m log m). The key count is exponential in m and superpolynomial in that input size. A claim of 2^Omega(binary input length) would require a different encoding justification.
5. **Changing affine basis cannot fix this example.** An invertible affine coordinate transformation preserves intersections and equality/inequality of sets. Consequently it preserves the number and coefficients of distinct intersection keys. This does not cover nonlinear encodings, factoring, branching, or arbitrary projection. The star's satisfying assignments have full affine hull, so sound extraction of globally entailed affine equations also cannot reduce its dimension.
6. **Counting is stronger than the decision objective.** The proposed routine computes an exact count, so a polynomial bound would imply a polynomial SAT decider. Its exponential expansion on the star proves no lower bound for SAT, exact counting by other methods, or all representations. The star is monotone, has an immediate satisfying assignment, and has count 2^m+1 by conditioning on its hub. The fixed expansion wastes work on this family. Likewise the Tseitin outcome is the established ability to solve explicit linear constraints, not evidence of a general SAT algorithm or a quantum speedup.

## Scope of the conclusion

The two-key affine-coincidence example verifies a real compression mechanism: identical forbidden events cancel under the signed update. The star shows that shared variables alone do not supply those equalities. The connected star has unbounded hub occurrence, so it must not be presented as a bounded-degree obstruction. A future repair must change the operational rule and account for its costs; this review does not reject all possible repairs.
