# Solver frontier: proof existence, discovery, and escape routes

2026-09-11; S3061. Primary-source assessment as of this date, not a new proof or experiment. The [integrity boundary](../../INTEGRITY-CLAIMS.md) applies. This is a selective frontier map: no theorem or matched experiment below establishes a family hard for every known solver.

## The distinction that changes the research target

There are three different possible gaps: a restricted proof language cannot express a short refutation; a short refutation exists but finding it is hard or unproved; or particular implementations fail within a stated budget. An extension, alternate encoding, or specialized algorithm can remove the first barrier without solving the second. A finite timeout establishes neither.

| Approach | Established limitation | Important escape or unresolved boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution and resolution-only CDCL | Expansion/width yields exponential refutation-size lower bounds, including sparse random CNF and Tseitin families. | Applies only while the whole computation has a suitably efficient translation to resolution on the relevant encoding. Extra parity, extension, PB, or semantic preprocessing cannot silently be included. |
| Parity reasoning / Res(XOR) | Pure inconsistent linear systems are efficiently solved by Gaussian elimination, so they are unsuitable as a common obstruction. | Mixed nonlinear constraints remain; new parity decision and learned-clause selection must be charged. Simulation of a supplied proof is not its deterministic discovery. |
| Cutting Planes (CP), PB and branch-and-cut | Unrestricted CP has genuine size lower bounds; random k-SAT bounds with logarithmic k are established. Low-weight Stabbing Planes also has size lower bounds. | Do not transfer the logarithmic-k theorem to random 3-SAT, or low-weight SP bounds to unrestricted SP. PB extensions escape some plain-CP restrictions. |
| Polynomial Calculus (PC/PCR) | Degree and monomial-size bounds hold for specified fields, bases and encodings. Functional PHP already has exponential PCR bounds. | Counting solves that PHP. Gaussian elimination escapes parity examples over the matching field. Degree, monomial count and coefficient bit size are different resources. |
| Sum of Squares (SOS) | Random CSP pseudoexpectations block refutation at quantified degrees/densities. | A degree lower bound concerns that hierarchy/encoding, not every algorithm. An arbitrary extension or nonlinear reformulation needs its own simulation argument. |
| Frege / Extended Frege (EF) | General superpolynomial size lower bounds remain a major open problem. | The lack of bounds is not an efficient solver. Some random formulas hard for resolution already have short threshold-Frege proofs. |
| Proof discovery / automatability | Resolution, CP, and several algebraic systems are NP-hard to automate in the precise shortest-proof-relative sense. | This is a worst-case conditional obstruction, not a lower bound on every heuristic or a proof that a particular average-case family is hard. |

## Exact source scopes behind the matrix

**Resolution.** [Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, JACM 2001](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/SatSem05/Papers/BensassonWidgerson01.pdf) relate refutation size S and width w for an n-variable CNF of initial width w0: the size lower bound has the form `S >= exp(Omega((w-w0)^2/n))`. Their applications include standard expansion-based examples. The statement bounds proof representations, not all algorithms. [Vinyals et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09422) additionally show that standard CDCL learning restrictions can create overhead even when resolution proofs are short. Nondeterministic heuristic choices in simulation results must not be read as an efficient way to find those choices.

**Current parity escape.** [Beame and Sun, SAT 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5), published July 16, give CDCL(XOR) with parity branching, propagation, and linear-clause learning. Their polynomial simulation of Res(XOR) explicitly allows nondeterministic decisions and restarts. Selected native-XNF benchmarks and their CNF Tseitin results are evidence for a concrete escape, not universal performance. Ordinary clauses plus separate XOR equations are also a narrower representation than arbitrary disjunctions of parity equations.

**CP and extensions.** [Fleming, Pankratov, Pitassi and Robere, revised 2018](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2017/045/) prove exponential CP size bounds for random k-SAT with k logarithmic in the variable count in the stated unsatisfiable regime. This supplies a real CP frontier, but does not give the needed fixed-width random-3SAT comparison. [Fleming et al., ToC June 2026, Theorems 1.3-1.5](https://www.theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v022a002/v022a002.pdf) give CP refutations of prime-field linear-system CNF encodings of size `|F|^{O(log m)}`, low-weight SP size lower bounds, and a semantic-CP depth lower bound for Tseitin. Quasipolynomial size is not polynomial; depth is not size; these results still invalidate a blanket claim that parity defeats counting. The paper states unrestricted-SP size lower bounds as open. [Gocht and Nordstrom, AAAI 2021, Section 4](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/16494/16301) already certify parity with pseudo-Boolean reasoning and extension variables. A fresh mixture of Gaussian elimination and counting is therefore not itself a frontier advance.

**Algebraic representation matters.** [Miksa and Nordstrom, CCC 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01358) prove functional-PHP PC/PCR degree and size bounds via expansion; this is compatible with efficient counting proofs. A particularly important current warning is [Alekseev, ToC June 2026, Theorem 1.1 and introduction](https://www.theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v022a004/v022a004.pdf): the exponential rational-bit-size lower bound for extension-PC with square roots concerns the Binary Value Principle. The paper explicitly says this is not the polynomial-size CNF translation needed for a Frege/EF lower bound; the principle also has short SOS proofs. It is not a common hard CNF or a solved EF frontier.

**SOS.** [Kothari, Mori, O'Donnell and Witmer, Theorems 1.2 and 1.4](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/papers/csp-sos-lower-bounds.pdf) quantify the random-CSP degree/density/refutation tradeoff. For random 3-OR, the pairwise-uniform satisfying distribution gives a weak-refutation degree obstruction of order `n/(Delta^2 log Delta)` in their density regime, up to constants/polylogarithmic formulations, where m=Delta*n. Thus m of order n^(7/5) still requires growing degree, about n^(1/5) modulo logarithms, rather than constant-degree SOS. Their theorem does not forbid a different proof search mechanism. Field-sensitive PC results must be cited separately; no unverified all-field/all-basis random-3SAT size statement is inferred here.

**Automatability.** For a proof system Q, the usual bound is time polynomial in `|F| + shortest_Q_refutation_size(F)`, not merely polynomial in |F|. [Atserias and Muller](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02991) rule this out for resolution unless P=NP. [Goos, Koroth, Mertz and Pitassi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08037) give the corresponding CP hardness. [De Rezende et al., STOC 2021, Theorem 1.1](https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/AutomatingAlgebraic_STOC.pdf) use one reduction giving a simultaneous size gap for resolution, Nullstellensatz, PC and Sherali-Adams, over the stated fields/encodings. SOS is explicitly excluded from that main theorem. This simultaneous result is a useful genuine overlap, but neither adds CP/EF automatically nor identifies a universally hard natural benchmark.

## A sharper frontier than another toy inference rule

Let D(n,m) be the distribution obtained by choosing m distinct clauses uniformly from the `8*binom(n,3)` signed clauses on distinct variables. At m=O(n^(7/5)), drawing independent clauses instead changes events with probability o(1), since the collision probability is O(m^2/n^3)=o(1). This distribution comparison does not license planted, conditioned-SAT, or arbitrary benchmark substitutions.

[Feige, Kim and Ofek (FKO), FOCS 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/unsat.pdf) establish polynomial-size UNSAT witnesses with high probability for m>=C*n^(7/5), for sufficiently large C, with a subexponential witness-search procedure. [Muller and Tzameret](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3970) show that these random CNFs even have polynomial-size TC0-Frege refutations. This is therefore a **discovery gap**, not absence of short proofs across the major systems.

The gap has a specific combinatorial core. [Tzameret, Section 4.1, Definition 3 and Theorem 7](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~itzamere/AutFKO.pdf) isolates a sufficiently large collection of inconsistent even clause-tuples with bounded overlap; imbalance and certified spectral estimates are the efficiently computable parts. Efficient interpolation for a system proving the associated 3XOR principle is a sufficient alternative route, but is itself a substantial missing hypothesis. Merely demonstrating new Gaussian or PB inferences does not provide this search operation.

A bounded current-status check found [Caragiannis, Gravin and Jiang, ESA 2025, introduction pp.103:3-4](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol351-esa2025/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.103/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.103.pdf) still identifying sufficiently large `C*n^(3/2)` as the best-known general efficient random-3SAT refutation density, via Feige-Ofek. Targeted 2025/2026 primary searches found no replacement by September 11, 2026. This is a bounded literature finding, not an exhaustive negative theorem.

**Best-supported theoretical question:** can a deterministic polynomial-time procedure, given only F from D(n,ceil(C*n^(7/5))), construct and verify an FKO witness with probability 1-o(1) over F, while never reporting UNSAT for a satisfiable input? Every failed tuple search, packing/overlap step, spectral precision and verification cost must be included. A sound refuter with a different certificate would also close the larger gap. No finder, intermediate mechanism, or tractability evidence from our previous prototypes is claimed. This is a substantial existing open problem, not low-hanging fruit or an authorized next experiment.

## What this would mean for P versus NP

An average-case refutation improvement at a specified density does not decide arbitrary SAT, nor prove P!=NP. A uniform deterministic polynomial-time decision algorithm for every CNF would prove P=NP. In the opposite direction, [Cook-Reckhow](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf) relates NP=coNP to the existence of some polynomially bounded propositional proof system. Thus disproving polynomial boundedness for every such system would separate NP from coNP and hence P from NP. A lower bound for one fixed system, even EF, does not by itself establish that universal statement. [Pich and Santhanam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08163) give conditional routes from EF lower bounds with substantial extra hypotheses; they are not unconditional implications.
