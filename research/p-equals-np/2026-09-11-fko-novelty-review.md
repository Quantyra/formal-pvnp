## Strongest significance concern

The n^(1/5) cap has a real motivation: it matches the desired FKO tuple support scale. But it bounds all explored clauses rather than the final extracted tuple. [Wu et al., Section IV.3](https://users.ics.aalto.fi/orponen/papers/unsat_2013.pdf) grows until signed inconsistency and then prunes. Therefore S3066 does not settle the published policy's behavior or prove that larger exploration cannot yield a short useful core. Memoryless restarts and endpoint-independent ties further narrow its model.

The adaptive crossing proof is more than the global absence of very short tuples: it remains useful when the cap constant exceeds the excluded global-size constant, and it controls many restarts while paying input exceptions once. Nevertheless no established open question in exactly this capped model was found by this review. This limits the current contribution case.

One additional observation: the proof's exposure and crossing estimates appear to rely on boundary selection from the coarse transcript rather than on maximality of overlap itself. This suggests a potentially broader information-restricted exploration formulation. That broader theorem has not been stated and reviewed here; it must not be included in present claims or silently used to strengthen significance.

A valid new negative theorem can be publishable without an improved solver. Conversely, a new exact combination of restrictions does not automatically have research significance. Present evidence supports retaining S3066 as the strongest candidate for further priority assessment, not declaring either publishability or impossibility of publication. No manuscript is authorized by this review.

## Meta-graph and holography scope check

The ledger distinguishes verified transformations, restricted failures, unresolved operations and speculative possibilities. It explicitly separates its own research nodes from the large lifted-state graph. Negative closed returns yield individual tuples, not a load-controlled family by themselves.

The signed double-cover formula agrees with [Brown et al., Section 5, equation 25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.0505). Keeping or flipping a parity bit maps negative closed walks to opposite-sheet paths; a change to the bit's plus/minus basis separates unsigned and signed sectors. This is established linear algebra, not an algorithmic speedup. Quantum evolution still requires implementation, preparation, time, precision and output guarantees; the killed classical chain is not unitary.
