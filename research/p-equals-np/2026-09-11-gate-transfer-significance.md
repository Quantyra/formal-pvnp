## Contract that actually improved

For an arbitrary binary n-by-M matrix with column weight three, sign vector, support cap k and nonnegative rational prices, S3073 constructs an exact minimum-price odd zero-incidence support oracle. Its explicit half-description domain has size

    N <= M^{O(log k)} 2^{O(k)} max(1,Delta)^{floor(k/2)} poly(n,M,k),

where Delta is maximum row weight. S3076 uses polynomial-gate indexed decoding, rather than an exponential lookup table, to search the weighted opposite-side keys in N^(6/7) times polynomial factors in original input size, price bits and error parameters. Quantum working space is N^(2/7) times polynomial factors. Adaptive pricing and rational packing have polynomial overhead; classical exact certificate checks preserve soundness on every input even when quantum search fails.

This is ordinary bounded-fan-in **active-gate** complexity. It is neither a depth-times-width result nor a physical fault-tolerance resource estimate. The exponent 6/7 and prefix architecture come from [Jaques-Schrottenloher, SAC 2020, Section 3.5](https://sacworkshop.org/SAC20/files/preproceedings/04-QuantumSearch.pdf). The candidate contribution is the arbitrary-key access transfer and its sparse weighted application, not a new generic collision exponent.

At M = ceil(C n^(7/5)), Delta = O(n^(2/5)) with high probability, and a sufficiently large source-supported cap k = O(n^(1/5)), the reviewed gate logarithm is at most (6/35) k log n + O(k) + O((log n)^2), with leading space logarithm (2/35) k log n. These are upper bounds with the same chosen k, not optimized numerical exponents in n. Original FKO robust-witness slack and charged directed spectral precision give a random-CNF refutation application. They do not give worst-case completeness at this short cap.

The July 2026 refutation and quantum theorem statements were re-opened for this audit; arXiv lists only v1, submitted July 31. They are preprint claims with inspected definitions and theorem scopes, not independently audited complete proofs. Their low-sign-degree lower bounds do not establish hardness for all algorithms. Broader CSP scope also need not match the iid random-literal model used here.

The relevant distinction is not merely classical versus quantum. Minimum-distance search, noise decoding, strong value certification, one odd dependency, exact weighted pricing and load-controlled packing are different outputs. A code correcting a promised noise distribution efficiently need not find its exact minimum distance, and a classical algorithm for a quantum code is not thereby a quantum algorithm. The [earlier focused half-list audit](2026-09-11-half-list-novelty.md) records sparse-ISD, separator and trellis alternatives and their unrecovered hypotheses. No stronger matching theorem was verified in this bounded audit; no claim that none exists is justified.

## Direct relationship to the Chief Scientist's objective

The average-case refuter can be useful quantum algorithm research without being an established P-versus-NP stepping stone. There is no reduction in this record carrying arbitrary SAT instances into the successful random ensemble while preserving a usable decision guarantee. The current algorithm is subexponential, not polynomial; it may return no certificate on difficult inputs. An upper bound for this task proves no unrestricted lower bound. Even a polynomial-time quantum SAT algorithm would address NP versus BQP, not by itself imply classical P = NP.

One precise stronger target with a logical link is a **single uniform deterministic polynomial-time procedure that outputs a sound, polynomially checkable refutation for every unsatisfiable CNF within a known polynomial bound**. Run it for that bound and verify: an accepted refutation means UNSAT; its absence means SAT by the hypothesized completeness. This would put SAT in P and hence imply P = NP. Our result supplies neither all-input completeness, polynomial runtime, nor deterministic classical discovery. This target is stated to expose the missing obligations, not to rebrand the present bound as progress toward satisfying them.

No concrete mechanism for that stronger target is supported by this audit. Research selection for the P-versus-NP objective therefore remains unresolved. A further reduction of the current n^(1/5) log n exponent, or even polynomial random-FKO discovery, would still require a separate worst-case bridge. The appropriate disposition is to retain the reviewed algorithmic result, park the quantum-method route, and avoid automatically generating another sampler, implementation or novelty loop.
