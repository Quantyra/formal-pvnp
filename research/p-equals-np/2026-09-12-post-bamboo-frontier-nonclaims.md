## Numeric costs are not input bit costs

The author's Subset Sum input is a list of positive binary integers and a
target, with full encoding length L. The standard base-ten SAT reduction
has polynomial bit length but potentially exponential numeric target and
universe. Time near-linear in numeric t, p or u, or dependent on the number
of output sums, is not automatically polynomial in L or log t. The current
deterministic baseline is credited; merely derandomizing an already
deterministic guarantee is not presented as the advance.

Only O(log W) modulus-doubling levels does not bound total work. The
records charge all visited fibers, repeated work, bit arithmetic, failed
branches, witness reconstruction and regularity-certificate construction.
Running a full modular algorithm at the final huge modulus retains numeric
dependence. Keeping the modulus small leaves the exact carry question.
Polynomial space is separately distinguished from polynomial time.

The exact split query is `U_r intersect (t-V_(t-r)) != empty`. Separate
residue witnesses need not give one compatible integer target witness;
separate modular answers cannot be joined by an unproved common-witness
assumption. Item-disjointness is retained. Minimum/maximum/gcd information
and one witness per fiber do not certify the intersection. Dense structure
requires its actual quantitative hypotheses, not merely many witnesses.
Conditioned fibers are not free unconstrained instances of the original
algorithm. Progression membership and lifting certificates must be built,
not postulated after an uncharged enumeration.

The independent challenge correctly avoids treating a potentially large
reachable output as proof against every target-only algorithm. The issue
here is the absent selective completion-preserving construction and local
update law, not merely an unproved bound for a specified construction.
That is an honest selection limitation; no general lower bound
is inferred from failure to find it in this bounded search.

## Exact complexity implications and remaining nonclaims

A complete deterministic polynomial **bit-time** exact algorithm on all
the specified SAT-reduction images, including sound NO decisions, would
imply P=NP. The intake states this conditional implication; it does not
claim the required algorithm, invariant or proof. An NP-hard reduction or
a conditional fine-grained lower bound is not used as an unconditional
reason such research cannot succeed.

The threshold candidate instead targets an improved exponential-time exact
#SAT algorithm for a specified circuit class. Faster exponential counting
does not itself give P=NP or P!=NP. A circuit lower-bound consequence would
require a separately checked algorithm-to-lower-bound theorem, including
its uniformity and size/depth quantifiers. No such new consequence is
claimed. Approximate coloring, local consistency, probabilistic internal
representations and a constant error bound do not alone certify exact
UNSAT or preservation of a unique witness.
