# Quantum and structural alternatives at the SAT frontier

2026-09-11; S3061. Frontier research under the [integrity boundary](../../INTEGRITY-CLAIMS.md), not a proof increment or experiment. Primary sources were checked on this date; coverage is focused, not exhaustive. **No common instance family has been established here as hard for all known solvers.** Quantum algorithms provide real, scoped improvements, while their known guarantees do not provide a universal efficient SAT escape. Failure to find such an escape is not a lower bound.

## Quantum approaches: what actually remains missing

| Approach | Established or reported result | Missing bridge to a collective-hardness claim |
|---|---|---|
| Quantum backtracking | Montanaro's Theorem 1.1 detects a solution with error at most delta in O(sqrt(T n) log(1/delta)) predicate/heuristic evaluations, given a valid tree bound T. Theorem 1.2 supplies a witness-search variant. | T belongs to a specified backtracking procedure. Charge reversible predicate/heuristic evaluation and construction, including randomness representation. Whole-tree detection is not automatically faster than classical first-witness search. This is neither a simulation of every modern CDCL solver nor a proof that explicit CNFs require exponential quantum time. [Published theorem, pp. 3–4](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v014a015/v014a015.pdf). |
| Unstructured search | Black-box search yields quadratic improvements; query/oracle lower bounds constrain that access model. | A CNF is explicit structure, not merely an oracle over assignments. A black-box lower bound cannot rule out exploiting its clauses. [Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9701001). Grover over the full cube is also an inadequate sole classical-comparison baseline. |
| Practical structured quantum search | Brehm–Weggemans compare compiled backtracking/Grover estimates with a strong classical solver on a tunably structured distribution. Their published 2026 analysis finds a sharply restricted advantage region; results depend on T-depth versus T-count and structure. | This is a distribution/implementation/resource study, not a theorem against all quantum algorithms. Section 4.2 discusses improved search variants; Section 4.3 identifies fitted medians and qualitative limits. It supports demanding matched total costs, not declaring the problem universally quantum-hard. [Final v3, Sections 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13274v3). |
| QAOA | The random-8-SAT resource study uses an instance-average success model, empirically fitted depth dependence and compiled fault-tolerant costs, compared with classical heuristic timing. Its Section II.1 explicitly calls the quantum solver incomplete. | The research gap is robust per-instance success and total algorithmic cost for a precisely specified distribution, including parameter acquisition and difficult tails. A finite median fit, inverse ensemble-average success, and expected individual runtime are different functionals. This does not settle worst-case SAT or certify UNSAT. [Omanakuttan et al., Sections II and IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01897v1). Prior local audits remain in force; deferred S3053 amplification is not reopened. |
| Adiabatic evolution | Jansen–Ruskai–Seiler Theorem 3 bounds tracking error using a separated spectral band, initial state in that band, Hamiltonian derivatives, and inverse gap factors. | A SAT encoding alone supplies none of the needed uniform bounds along the path. One must specify an efficiently prepared start, implementable path/schedule, relevant band and precision; control every factor of the theorem, including band multiplicity where present. Degeneracy at a satisfiable endpoint requires care. A small gap for one path does not exclude another path or nonadiabatic algorithm, and a final integer energy spacing does not bound the intermediate gap. [Theorem 3 and Section VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0603175). |

For any measurement-based or variational proposal, also distinguish finding a witness from returning a bounded-error decision and from producing a separately checkable UNSAT proof. Failed sampling is not a certificate. A uniform polynomial-time bounded-error SAT algorithm would establish NP contained in BQP, not by itself P=NP. No such general result is established by the sources above.

The objective must remain **global**. If H counts violated clauses, exact expectation below epsilon implies probability of a violation at most epsilon, since every violating assignment has H>=1. But a small *fraction* of violated clauses, or a good MaxSAT approximation ratio, need not provide a satisfying assignment at all. If all observed states violate one clause, normalized energy is 1/m and global SAT success is zero. Likewise, finding a positive-energy assignment on an UNSAT input does not certify that its energy is optimal. These are elementary distinctions, not new impossibility theorems.

## A current alternative deserves attention, without declaring a new mechanism

Lu et al.'s **published 2026** enhanced one-in-three-SAT study uses a classical parity relaxation to restrict quantum dynamics. Its final Methods include both subspace-aware random sampling and a subspace-aware random-walk classical baseline; these are absent from a simplistic comparison against unreduced brute force. The authors explicitly leave specialized classical heuristics as an open comparison. The evidence is finite scaling on specified random positive one-in-three instances, not uniform random signed 3-CNF or an all-input guarantee. [Final article, Results, Discussion and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01007-8.pdf).

The relevant open comparison is whether a residual quantum-search benefit survives stronger classical treatment of the **same affine-constrained nonlinear residual** and full costs. The reduction is already published; proposing it again would not be a frontier advance. “2-SAT within an affine subspace” does not inherit the ordinary 2-SAT algorithm: substituting quotient coordinates generally turns individual original literals into affine functions. Dropping the subspace changes the problem. The exact general problem is expressive enough to encode one-in-three constraints. This interpretation is our mathematical scope check, not a claim that the article proves a new classical lower bound.

This is a genuine source-identified uncertainty, but no new theorem, superior classical method or quantum advantage is derived here. An investigation would need a substantive idea beyond the already available affine reduction. No new benchmark or algorithm is launched.

## Structural escape routes that a shared example must survive

**Small decomposition width is an established escape.** Slivovsky–Szeider's incidence-treewidth model-counting algorithm has exponential factor 2^t, improving the older 4^t factor, with polynomial factors and a supplied tree decomposition. Counting also decides SAT. Charge decomposition discovery, representation size and integer arithmetic; a large observed heuristic decomposition is not a certified minimum-width lower bound. Large width does not itself prove all algorithms hard. [SAT 2020 paper](https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/tr/ac-tr-20-006.pdf).

**A small strong backdoor is another escape**, if all restrictions fall into a class solved by the chosen subsolver and the backdoor can be obtained at charged cost. Enumerating its assignments costs 2^b times residual work. Merely finding a promising assignment, or a weak backdoor on SAT inputs, does not settle UNSAT. This distinction and all-subset search already occur in [Williams–Gomes–Selman, Definitions 2.3–2.4 and Algorithm 4.1](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/03/Papers/168.pdf); see also the locally checked [S3059 primary comparison](2026-09-11-cnf-case-discovery-sources.md). This note adds no new backdoor theorem.

Bounded clause length, bounded occurrence and bounded treewidth are different restrictions. Tovey's exact-three-distinct-variables setting is always satisfiable at maximum occurrence three, whereas allowing occurrence four gives NP-completeness. Neither claim applies indiscriminately to clauses of length *at most* three. [Author's publication summary and paper listing](https://sites.gatech.edu/craig-tovey/publications/). Thus bounded degree can create an escape at particular bounds, but “bounded degree” alone is no proof of tractability. The uniform random model below has constant average occurrence, not a fixed maximum-occurrence promise.

Classical exact exponential algorithms also exploit more than unstructured search. A July 2026 PPSZ-analysis preprint reports an improved general-3-SAT bound, O*(1.307031578^n). We checked its abstract and version identity, not its rational certificate; it is a current comparison lead rather than a locally audited theorem. Do not present old Schoning or full-cube enumeration as the strongest relevant classical bound. [Jiang–Cai preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10697).

## Adversarial screen of near-threshold random 3-SAT

A precise *candidate comparison distribution*, not a universal obstruction, is: n variables; m=round(4.267 n) clauses sampled independently; each clause selects three distinct variable indices uniformly and three independent uniform signs. Repeated clauses are permitted under this definition. No instances were generated. Distinct-clause sampling, Poisson clause counts and literal sampling with replacement are different finite distributions and must be named if used instead.

The value about 4.267 is a statistical-physics prediction, not a rigorously identified threshold for k=3. The proved threshold identification of Ding–Sly–Sun applies to sufficiently large k and cannot simply be substituted at k=3. [Mertens–Mezard–Zecchina cavity calculation](https://www-e.ovgu.de/mertens/publications/sat1.pdf), [Ding–Sly–Sun main theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.0650). This focused current check found no source establishing the exact k=3 value; it is not an exhaustive priority search.

Most importantly, backtracking survey propagation already gives experimental evidence for finding solutions throughout the random-3-SAT satisfiable phase, with reported million-variable experiments. The extrapolated algorithmic threshold nearly coincides with the predicted satisfiability threshold. This is not a rigorous polynomial-time theorem, but it directly defeats the blanket description “near-threshold instances defeat all strongest heuristics.” UNSAT certification and hard tails remain separate. [Marino et al., Figure 3 and accompanying analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms12996?error=cookies_not_supported).

Three population distinctions are mandatory:

- **Unconditioned versus SAT-conditioned:** conditioning removes UNSAT inputs and changes distributions. A search algorithm tested only after another solver establishes SAT has not paid for general decision or certification. A fixed density above the threshold also admits the trivial high-probability label “UNSAT” under a distributional-error metric; require sound certificates or a per-input correctness condition before treating this as refutation.
- **Planted versus uniform-conditioned:** ordinary planting weights a formula by its number Z(F) of satisfying assignments. Elementary derivation: sum the equal conditional clause probabilities over each possible planted satisfying assignment. Uniform sampling conditioned on Z(F)>0 weights each satisfiable formula equally. Quiet-planting equivalence requires an actual theorem for the selected distribution and regime, not an assumption.
- **Finite timing versus worst case:** report preprocessing and certification, hardware/resources, restart policy, timeouts and censored data, success quantiles and seeds. A median or finite portfolio timeout supplies neither an all-input bound nor resistance to algorithms outside that portfolio. Formula size after a reduction and shared preprocessing benefits must be charged symmetrically.

Pure XOR/Tseitin, basic pigeonhole, disjoint constant-size contradictions, Horn/2-CNF and bounded-width decompositions are unsuitable as collective obstructions: Gaussian, counting, decomposition or dedicated polynomial methods are known escapes. Our S3055–S3060 failures against particular policies do not repair this deficiency. An NP-complete encoding is also not a guarantee that its sampled instances are difficult.

## Specialized escape from the concrete competition candidate

The parallel practice audit identified `SDP_136_18_712.sanitized.cnf.xz` as an all-configuration timeout candidate. Its measured competition result belongs to that audit; it is not independently reproduced here. **A syndrome-decoding CNF is especially unsuitable for inferring universal hardness from a CDCL-heavy panel.** Classical information-set decoding (ISD) attacks the underlying linear system plus weight constraint directly. Esser–Bellini provide a framework and practical cost estimates covering several ISD variants; the original estimator now directs users to the maintained CryptographicEstimators project. These are algorithm-cost estimates, not certified lower bounds. [PKC 2022 paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/1243.pdf), [current primary implementation](https://github.com/Crypto-TII/CryptographicEstimators).

Quantum ISD is also a genuine alternative to generic CNF search: Kachigar–Tillich and Kirshanova use quantum search/walks and structured subproblems. Do not transfer a generic asymptotic exponent to this finite file. Matrix dimensions and rank, code rate, target weight, solution multiplicity and algorithmic parameters matter. [Kachigar–Tillich, problem definitions and algorithms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00263), [Kirshanova](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00714). A current 2026 circuit-level comparison reports that elimination costs can erase the benefit of quantum walks over simpler quantum search for its practical cryptographic parameter sets; only its primary repository abstract was accessible in this check, so no numeric result or theorem is imported. [Quantum Walks for Collision-Based Information Set Decoding](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1319006).

The necessary missing evidence is the original H, syndrome s, exact weight equality versus upper-bound condition, rank, generator promise, and reversible map between original witnesses and the sanitized CNF. **No parameters are inferred from the filename.** All specialized classical and quantum methods are untested for this exact artifact in this note. If promised satisfiable, ISD witness search has a different task from unrestricted certified UNSAT; unsuccessful randomized decoding does not establish nonexistence. Memory, list construction/access, reversible elimination, oracle construction and error correction must be charged before any quantum time comparison. This is an identified missing comparison, not a claim that ISD solves this file quickly.

**Disposition:** the concrete SDP timeout is a scoped measured candidate with an explicit untested specialized escape; near-threshold random 3-SAT is a different comparison distribution with known strong heuristic evidence against blanket hardness. Neither can be substituted for a theoretical sparse-random-3-SAT proof-discovery family. The quantum-specific missing bridge is a proved or well-supported global-success/resource advantage after matching the strongest relevant structural/classical treatment. The 2026 affine-restricted one-in-three study identifies another live comparison, but this screen establishes no common hard family, new research mechanism, or P-versus-NP progress.
