## Decision criteria and scope

A target qualifies only if it has an exact resource-bounded statement, a valid implication chain, and a concrete mathematical reason from our work to expect progress on its missing premise. Being an open problem or having a short path in the [implication map](2026-09-11-complexity-implication-map.md) is insufficient. A restricted or intermediate result can qualify without immediately resolving P versus NP, provided it has a specified extension mechanism toward the target and an evidence-backed local opening. NONE below reflects the absence of that supported mechanism, not a requirement that every first step settle the whole problem. We examined exactly three classical mechanisms; the quantum bridge is independently assessed in the [companion screen](2026-09-11-pvnp-quantum-target-screen.md). No experiment, Lean theorem, formal build or new force route was executed.

All running times below refer to uniform deterministic computation on finite binary inputs unless explicitly stated otherwise. Counts of geometric nodes, real-number operations, proof lines and circuit gates are not interchangeable with total bit work. A restricted-family theorem must retain that restriction; it cannot silently cover arbitrary CNFs or all proof systems.

## 1. Structured classical SAT and the height representation

A direct sufficient contract would be: there exist fixed deterministic algorithms and a constant c such that, for every CNF F of encoding length N, they construct and process a finite representation in at most N^c bit operations and output SAT exactly when F has a satisfying assignment. Every constructed state, branch, unsuccessful rewrite, equality test, auxiliary object and intermediate integer is charged. This implies SAT in P and hence P=NP. Without a specified new representation invariant or operation, the contract is a restatement of the goal rather than a research lead.

A more informative progress claim requires a fixed algorithm first. For example, the existing Horn-guided engine used disjoint frontier cubes with integer potential W initially at most 2^n. Its proposed claim quantified a single polynomial p(N): on every nonterminal block of every run, at most p(N) bit work reduces W by a factor at most 1-1/p(N), with sound SAT/UNSAT termination when no positive frontier remains. That would give polynomial total work. Merely calling W a height or assigning successive nodes levels proves no such decrease, construction bound or branch bound. Binary search on a height is useful only if its threshold predicate is already efficiently decidable without a SAT oracle.

The [classical consolidation](2026-09-08-consolidation-classical-audit.md) and [specified attack](2026-09-08-consolidation-attack-spec.md) already found that completed NO runs of this particular engine produce ordinary resolution refutations of size bounded by charged work. The uniform progress claim therefore fails on the expanding-graph Tseitin families covered by [Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, Theorem 4.4/Corollary 4.5](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/SatSem05/Papers/BensassonWidgerson01.pdf). These have exponential resolution size, with polynomially encoded input. This excludes that claim for that engine, not every height representation or SAT algorithm.

The surviving Horn closure operation has classical linear-time precedents in [Dowling and Gallier](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/~cis5110/Dowling-Gallier-Horn-sat.pdf). The record supplies no new general-CNF choice operation. Later [holographic](2026-09-09-holographic-checkpoint-review.md) and [Hodge transfer](2026-09-09-ns-hodge-transfer-audit.md) audits do not reopen the old ACTIVE annotations: one specific common signature basis fails, and geometric transfer lacks the required effective bridge. Neither yields a polynomial all-input height evaluator.

**Stop rule:** do not rerun the old examples or relabel the potential. Reconsider only after an explicitly defined operation changes the proved simulation boundary and comes with a noncircular cost/progress invariant. Adding extension variables may escape ordinary resolution, but does not establish efficient discovery, short proofs or search. No such operation was found here.

## 2. Algorithms to circuit lower bounds

A direct separation target is: there exists a language L in NP such that, for every integer k, infinitely many input lengths n require Boolean circuits of size greater than n^k to compute L on all n-bit inputs. Circuits are unrestricted nonuniform Boolean circuits, not monotone circuits or a bounded-depth class. This is L outside P/poly; because P is contained in P/poly, it implies P != NP. Our repository has not proved this premise.

The algorithmic mechanism is real. [Williams, Theorem 1.3](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/acc-lbs-journal-final.pdf) treats a circuit class C closed under composition and containing AC0. It supplies a constant k>0 such that solving SAT on n-input size-n^c C-circuits in O(2^n/n^k) time for every c implies NTIME[2^n] lacks polynomial-size C-circuits. The established ACC result is an instance of this program. Even selecting unrestricted circuits here gives an exponential-time-class circuit lower bound, not automatically an NP lower bound or P != NP. It would be incorrect to transfer the conclusion's class label downward.

The sharper [Murray-Williams easy-witness results](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/easy-witness-nqp.pdf), including Theorems 1.1-1.2, show that the program is not intrinsically confined to NEXP. Their stronger gap-circuit algorithm hypotheses have different input sizes and nondeterministic time bounds. In particular, an NP conclusion with the form 'for every k there exists a language L_k in NP requiring more than n^k gates' is not by itself 'there exists L in NP outside P/poly': the order of quantifiers matters. We do not adopt any of these unproved algorithm premises as local evidence.

No existing local Horn, height or tensor routine has a proved speedup meeting a named circuit-analysis theorem on all circuits of its required size. Converting a circuit to CNF introduces variables and can destroy an exponential-time saving measured in the original n. A finite SAT benchmark or a fast tractable fragment does not discharge this issue.

**Stop rule:** no lower-bound campaign from a boxed implication alone. Require a concrete circuit class, algorithm, size regime, sound gap/decision specification and measured-in-the-right-input theorem before assessing its consequence. No such new algorithm was identified, so we select no intermediate target here either.

## 3. Proof complexity and the universal-system bridge

In relational form, a Cook-Reckhow system R is a polynomial-time decidable proof relation that is sound and complete for propositional tautologies. Polynomial boundedness means: there exists a polynomial p such that every tautology phi has an R-proof pi with |pi|<=p(|phi|). The desired universal negative statement is: for every such R and every polynomial p, some tautology phi has no R-proof of length at most p(|phi|). By [Cook-Reckhow](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf), that statement is equivalent to NP != coNP and therefore implies P != NP. These equivalences do not make the universally quantified task easier.

A conditional bridge through one system S would require both a superpolynomial proof-length lower bound for S and the property that S polynomially simulates every Cook-Reckhow system. More explicitly, for each R there would be a polynomial-time translation of its proofs to S-proofs of the same formula, with polynomial length overhead depending on R. If a polynomially bounded R existed, this would make S polynomially bounded, contradicting the lower bound. The universal simulation premise is not established for the local systems or for Extended Frege. Short proofs, efficient checking and efficient proof discovery are distinct obligations.

Local resolution/switching infrastructure and named imported bounded-depth statements do not provide this bridge. Nor can one simply promote an Extended Frege lower bound to P != NP: [Pich-Santhanam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08163) develops explicitly conditional connections with substantial additional hardness/provability or learning assumptions. Those hypotheses are not supplied here. [Cook's correction](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/) also warns against confusing NP != coNP with P != NP in the original paper's corollary.

**Stop rule:** do not strengthen the system name or replace 'this system' by 'every system' without an actual translation theorem and its cost. No qualifying universal simulation, lower-bound family beyond the existing restricted evidence, or new transferable invariant was found.
