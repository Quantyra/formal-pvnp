# S3057: bounded affine feedback and its exact local-scope limit

2026-09-11. S3057 / S008 / E004. **Result:** the specified feedback rule is sound and reaches a fixed point in polynomial bit work for every fixed scope budget w. It repairs the paired-path family and a genuine two-round cascade. Nevertheless, for every fixed w there are arbitrarily large UNSAT binary CNFs on which it learns nothing. Increasing the scope to the cycle length exposes the contradiction. This is a precise limitation of this affine-summary rule, not a lower bound for SAT, 2-SAT, all local consistency or all affine reasoning. [Integrity boundary](../../INTEGRITY-CLAIMS.md).

## Selected operation, not a new solver label

Three possible changes were considered: (1) repeat Gaussian substitution alone; (2) **selected**, regroup bounded-rank affine-clause scopes and feed their local affine-hull consequences back into elimination; (3) retain and compose nonlinear implications across scopes. Operation 3 is not launched here. On binary clauses it has established 2-SAT/SCC precedents, so it is not automatically a new research direction.

The representation and broad feedback principle are known. Clauses are disjunctions of affine Boolean forms, often called XNF or linear clauses; [Andraschko-Danner-Kreuzer (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11786-024-00594-x) and [Beame-Sun (2026)](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5) already develop richer propagation and search. The [source comparison](2026-09-11-affine-feedback-sources.md) gives exact overlaps. Our mathematical attempt is the explicitly bounded local rule below and the all-length completeness counterexample, not a claim to have invented XNF or Gaussian feedback.

## Exact state and one simultaneous pass

Input consists of an explicit affine system Ax=b and an explicit list of clauses, each an OR of forms `a dot x XOR c`. Dot products and coefficients are over GF(2); a form is true when its value is one. Retain these original clauses throughout. A consequence of a local group may be added, but a nonlinear group is never replaced by its affine hull.

Gaussian elimination detects inconsistency or yields `x=p+Bz`. Choose z to be the original free-coordinate variables, with the particular solution zero in those free positions. This convention makes subsequent lifting explicit. Substituting into a literal keeps it as one affine form in z; it does not expand parity into CNF.

For each clause, falsity is a conjunction of affine equations. Compute the canonical augmented RREF of that entire falsity system in fixed current-coordinate order. An inconsistent falsity system means a tautological clause and is omitted. An empty falsity system means an always-false clause and certifies UNSAT. Otherwise its nonzero coefficient rows are a basis for its falsity constraints; constants remain attached to those rows.

Let V be the set of nonzero coefficient vectors appearing in these **canonical clause-falsity systems**, not necessarily the original literal vectors. Enumerate every distinct span generated by at most w vectors from V, using RREF for canonical keys. For each span W, collect every current clause whose entire falsity-row space is contained in W. Express each clause's equations in the fixed independent basis of W, including its affine offsets. Evaluate the collected conjunction on all `2^rank(W)` local patterns.

The local patterns are realizable by quotient assignments: independent rows defining W make the map `z -> Wz` surjective. If the local relation is empty, report UNSAT. Otherwise compute all affine equations holding on it and retain an independent defining basis of its affine hull. Lift these equations through W into z, and then into the original free-coordinate positions of x. The local constants already include the substitution offset p.

All groups in a pass use the same snapshot Ax=b and the same quotient. Add their derived equations simultaneously, row-reduce the augmented original-coordinate system, and only then recompute scopes. Constants and empty clauses are checked before nonzero-vector enumeration; they do not require a missing zero-span window. This defines an effective, fixed-coordinate rule. It does not claim access to all ambient low-rank subspaces, arbitrary semantic equivalence or a free global affine hull.

The possible returns are **UNSAT**, **SAT with a verified witness** when every residual clause has become tautological, and **OPEN** when no further equation is learned. OPEN means unresolved/unknown, never satisfiable. In particular, a nonempty local truth table is not used as a complete global witness.

## Soundness and rank-progress lemma

Every solution of the current whole formula satisfies each collected local conjunction. Therefore its local pattern lies in that conjunction's affine hull and satisfies every learned equation. Adding those equations while retaining all original clauses preserves exactly the original solution set. The affine parametrization and coordinate lift are bijective on the current affine solution space, so they do not introduce spurious solutions or discard valid ones. Empty local relations and contradictory learned equations soundly certify UNSAT.

A nontrivial learned equation is nonzero in the current free coordinates. Otherwise it would either be the already-true constant equation or an immediate contradiction. Because the W basis is independent, a nonzero local normal gives a nonzero quotient row. Such a row is not already implied by the current affine system: its linear functional varies on that system's kernel. Thus a productive consistent pass raises rank by at least one, even if many proposed equations are mutually dependent. Starting at rank r_0, at most n-r_0 consistent productive passes occur, followed by at most one final no-progress check. Contradiction may terminate earlier.

This proves finite monotone saturation, not completeness. No rank increase can recover nonlinear information that every local affine hull discards.

### Why retaining clauses matters

Exactly-one-of-three has solutions 100,010,001 and affine hull `x_1 XOR x_2 XOR x_3=1`. That hull also contains 111, which the original formula rejects. The checker learns the parity equation, retains the exclusions, and reaches OPEN rather than declaring the hull to be the solution set. This fixture tests a nontrivial learned hull with a surviving nonlinear residual, not just an OR whose hull is the whole space.

## Positive families and actual feedback

For the [S3056 paired paths](2026-09-11-decomposition-mechanism.md), supplied equalities x_i=y_i cause the positive and negative adjacent clauses to share one rank-two scope after substitution. Each pair is exactly the relation `x_i XOR x_(i+1)=1`. With w=2 the first pass derives the adjacent parity system, raises total rank from k to 2k-1, and the next normalization removes every clause. A witness follows from the remaining one-dimensional affine space. This is the known parity repair implemented as a general local feedback pass, without syndrome enumeration.

A smaller case shows why repeating the pass is operationally meaningful:

`(NOT a OR b) AND (a OR NOT b) AND (a OR c) AND (NOT b OR NOT c)`.

Initially the {a,b} scope derives a=b. The two other clauses are in different rank-two scopes and individually have full affine hull. After substitution, they occupy the same {b,c} quotient scope and imply b XOR c=1. The observed ranks are 0,1,2, followed by SAT. The new scope is exposed by the first equation; it was not evaluated on a secretly updated snapshot during the first pass.

## Infinite counterfamily and strict scope threshold

For every integer ell>=3, define F_ell using x and two disjoint lists a_1,...,a_(ell-1) and b_1,...,b_(ell-1). Its 2ell binary clauses encode

`x => a_1 => ... => a_(ell-1) => NOT x`,

`NOT x => b_1 => ... => b_(ell-1) => x`.

For example, the first chain uses `(NOT x OR a_1)`, the intermediate implication clauses and `(NOT a_(ell-1) OR NOT x)`. The second starts `(x OR b_1)` and ends `(NOT b_(ell-1) OR x)`.

The first chain forces x=0; the second forces x=1. Hence F_ell is UNSAT. It has 2ell-1 variables. Its undirected clause graph consists of two cycles, each with ell vertices, sharing only x. The shared vertex occurs four times and all other variables twice. This is an easy 2-SAT family, not a hard decision family.

### Forest full-affine-hull lemma

Consider any forest with one binary clause per edge, so each edge forbids exactly one assignment pair. Its satisfying relation has full affine hull on its vertices. To prove this, choose any vertex v. For each neighbor u, set u so that the incident clause is true regardless of v. Removing v leaves separate tree components, so these neighbor choices do not conflict. Extend each assignment outward: for every parent value, the edge excludes at most one child value, leaving at least one choice. Other components are satisfiable by the same argument. Both values of v now work with all other coordinates unchanged.

Thus for every v there are satisfying assignments whose difference is exactly the unit vector e_v. The differences span the entire coordinate space. This proves both local satisfiability and full affine hull, including isolated vertices. It is an elementary derivation about these clauses, not an appeal to a semantic oracle in the implemented rule.

### Applying the lemma to saturation

Initially there are no affine equations and every canonical falsity row is a coordinate unit vector. Consequently the generated spans of rank at most w are exactly coordinate windows of at most w variables. If w<ell, no such window contains a cycle; its collected clauses induce a forest. The lemma shows that every allowed local relation is satisfiable and has full affine hull. No equation is learned in the first pass, so the algorithm returns OPEN on the UNSAT input. Repeating the same fixed point cannot change this.

At w=ell, each complete cycle fits in a generated window. The first cycle's local hull contains x=0 and the second's contains x=1. Simultaneous feedback therefore derives a contradiction. The smaller internal path relation on each side is satisfiable, so the contradiction comes from combining the two learned root equations, not from assuming either cycle is itself UNSAT.

Therefore the exact threshold for this family is ell: every budget below ell stalls; budget ell detects inconsistency. Setting ell=w+1 gives adjacent examples, while letting ell grow above a fixed w gives arbitrarily large failing inputs for that same fixed-budget rule. The fixed-w saturation algorithm can be polynomial-time and incomplete without contradiction.

The loss is specifically the affine summary: local implications remain in the clauses but contribute no local linear equation. Ordinary implication reachability or SCC processing composes them and solves F_ell without these windows. The result does not constrain bounded-width CSP consistency in general, stronger XNF inference, all affine-aware solvers or P versus NP.

## Charged construction and bit complexity

Let n be the explicit active dimension, L the total number of input affine-form occurrences, q the number of input affine equations, and N the complete bit encoding size. Each form uses up to n coefficient bits plus its constant. Current original-coordinate equations are row-reduced, and original clauses remain fixed. Recomputing quotient forms keeps at most L forms; no exponential parity-to-CNF substitution occurs.

In one pass there are at most L occurring canonical coefficient rows. Generating and deduplicating all spans of at most w of them costs at most a polynomial factor times `(L+1)^w`; reducing each generating set and comparing dense RREF keys is charged. A scope then scans every residual clause for span membership. Evaluating its table costs at most `2^w` times its clause work. The actual implementation computes hull equations by enumerating up to `2^w` normals and testing up to `2^w` allowed points, so its hull cost is `4^w poly(w)`, not merely `2^w`.

A conservative total bound is initial polynomial Gaussian work plus

`(n+1) (L+1)^w [poly(N,w) + 4^w poly(w)]`.

This includes complete unsuccessful scans and final no-progress detection. Proposed equations from all scopes, row reduction of the batch, offsets, coefficient comparisons and coordinate lifting contribute polynomial factors. Rank progress alone was not used as a runtime proof. Storing all deduplicated scopes and candidate equations can take `(L+1)^w poly(n,w)` bits, plus the original input, matrix and local tables; the implementation makes no linear-memory claim.

For every fixed w these bounds are polynomial in the explicit input. If w grows, neither `(L+1)^w` nor `4^w` is free. Raising w to ell on F_ell is a correctness observation, not an efficient general strategy. Fixed coordinate conventions and canonical falsity systems define the tested scope collection; no arbitrary-coordinate invariance theorem is needed or claimed.

## Exact implementation evidence and interpretation

Run from the satellite root:

`python research/p-equals-np/2026-09-11-affine-feedback-mechanism.py`

The [stdlib script](2026-09-11-affine-feedback-mechanism.py) uses the previously reviewed [S3056 linear helpers](2026-09-11-decomposition-mechanism.py). The [results](2026-09-11-affine-feedback-mechanism.json) pin both files with SHA256-LF hashes. Eight controls and nine family cases passed. Controls cover constants, empty clauses, contradictory equations, affine offsets/tautologies, ordinary OR retention and exactly-one-of-three hull retention. Paired paths k=2..6 reach ranks [k,2k-1]; the three-variable cascade reaches [0,1,2]. F_ell for ell=3,4,5 is OPEN at w=ell-1 and UNSAT at w=ell; every tested smaller coordinate window's full affine hull is independently enumerated inside the checker. The final run took under one second.

Full assignment enumeration is used only to verify these small cases and learned-equation soundness. Saturation itself uses bounded local tables and linear algebra. The all-ell failure and threshold conclusions are derived above, not inferred from timings or finite extrapolation. No general complete SAT fallback is hidden behind OPEN.

**Strongest supported result:** bounded affine feedback has a charged polynomial fixed-point computation and a real multi-round repair, but affine-only local summaries miss globally composable implications on a precise infinite easy family. Increasing the scope resolves each example while changing the parameter-dependent cost. The representation and propagation components are established; novelty of this exact hierarchy formulation is unverified. This completes the selected discovery attempt without automatically launching another solver, proof campaign or publication.
