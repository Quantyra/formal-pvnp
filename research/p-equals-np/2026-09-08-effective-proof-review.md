## Finite-horizon initializer

The changed strategy is mathematically meaningful. An input-dependent parcel initialized extremely close to the exact invariant axis can survive any specified finite horizon without computing the stable sheet. It is a family of parcels, not one parcel surviving arbitrarily many turns from a single effectively specified positive radius.

The certificate implies the comparison inequality for the axial error because G(q,0,eta0)=0 and the segment from (0,eta0) to (X,eta) lies in the rectangle. Hence |G| is at most B|e|+CX. Multiplicative radial evolution gives X<=x0 exp(-wminus s), and convolution with exp(Bs) gives exactly the stated denominator B+wminus. Substituting the proposed initial tolerances bounds each contribution by delta/4. The first-exit argument closes: the radial upper face cannot be reached, the axial face retains slack, and bounded logarithmic derivatives prevent either positive coordinate from reaching zero in finite rescaled time. When q starts at q0, its lower bound uses q0 as written. Physical time is increasing because qL is positive; the endpoint has positive tau.

The angular integration has the correct direction: an upper bound on q produces a lower bound on q^(-h). The integer construction is conservative. Writing a=h*kminus, the conditions imply

    exp(a*S) >= 2^(p+c),
    2^(p+c)-1 >= (M+1)(1+8*a/fminus)-1 > 8*a*M/fminus.

Together with q0<=1 and 2*pi<8, this exceeds the required turns. Also 2^(-2*ceil(B*S))<=exp(-B*S), so the rational tolerance is safely smaller than the exponential one. The fixed-degree axis root has positive derivative on the isolating interval. Root bisection and dyadic output therefore have polynomial bit cost in log M for fixed certified rational parameters. This is a description/initializer bound, not a cost bound for positioning matter or simulating the flow.

The physical coordinate conversion needs the directed rounding and tolerance allocation stated in the draft. In particular, setting the approximate root is not itself a proof that physical coordinates satisfy the normalized certificate. The note correctly leaves this distinction explicit and allows its start time to depend on the input.

The actual-axis triangle estimates follow by expanding the exact trace formulas and using absolute-value bounds. Their strict margins are sufficient for qualitative continuity arguments. They do not certify any positive X-width. The evaluation of the leading axial equation at X=0 gives the stated derivative involving Zstar/L. That datum depends on the selected pressure profile; substituting the synthetic C=10 would be unsupported. The leading analytic norm conversion is correct: discard the extra coefficient denominator to bound the differentiated series by K/[20(1-Y/20)^2], and use Y<=1. Selecting K and transferring finitely many derivatives to the completed field remain separate obligations.

The current warning about the synthetic h=1/200 is necessary and correct. The broad bound h<1/100 does not license that numerical value as a complete admissible source choice. Neither a rational parameter selection nor numerical coefficient majorants have been constructed here.

## Localized controlled scalar

The transported profile satisfies D_t p=0 exactly. The flow preserves volume, so both normalization integrals and support volume are time-independent. This makes the nonlocal functional A_t bounded with norm at most Q on bounded scalars, and A_t[a*p]=a. Substitution into the full diffusion equation cancels chi*a*Delta p, leaving the amplitude ODE. The amplitude is a derived invariant mode, not an additional controller register storing a separately known bit. The primitive still requires spatial aggregation and redistribution and remains an imposed device component.

The inverse-flow derivative estimates are conservative and valid. Gronwall for the Hessian gives E^2 times an integral bounded by J2. For the third derivative it gives E^3 times an integral bounded by 3*J2^2+J3; indeed a sharper factor in the quadratic term is possible, but unnecessary. The chain-rule estimates for Delta p and its gradient follow with the conservative dimension factors used. The Eulerian plateau-registration condition follows from the inverse Lipschitz bound. Compact support inside a prescribed tube follows from the forward Lipschitz bound and the stated clearance condition; no uniform packet size near blowup is obtained.

A useful optional improvement is to replace K1 in E by the supremum of the Euclidean operator norm of sym(Du). For both forward and inverse first variations the skew part generates rotations and contributes zero to the derivative of squared Euclidean length. The same statement applies to each covariant input index of the inverse Hessian and third derivative: their homogeneous propagators have bounds E_strain^2 and E_strain^3. Variation of constants then gives the same conservative H2,H3 formulas with E_strain replacing E and the existing J2,J3 retained. This needs a consistent Euclidean tensor norm; it does not remove the inhomogeneous D2u and D3u terms. Rigid rotation illustrates the benefit: Du may be large while its symmetric part vanishes and the flow is an isometry. Quantifying strain for the completed field is a potential next certificate improvement, not a necessary repair to the current valid estimates. A large current E is a sufficient-bound artifact and must not be reported as a necessary exponential physical or computational cost.

The sup-error argument closes despite the nonlocal feedback. At a first upper or lower contact, the sensor error is bounded by Q times the current global sup norm. Wherever its diffusion pattern is nonzero, the mask equals one; elsewhere that term vanishes. Thus gamma>=2*chi*Q*P2 supplies the required absorption. Disturbances supported according to the mask use the remaining budget. The imperfect-sensor allocation also closes: feedback error consumes at most half, and source plus sensor error consume at most one quarter each. Whole-space bounded-solution barriers require the usual strict-barrier limiting argument, which the draft explicitly invokes. This is not an assumption of order preservation for the nonlocal operator.

The endpoint estimate combines amplitude relaxation with the invariant error ball and is below 1/8. Source magnitude, nominal spatial-gradient bounds, and the material derivative identity have the correct signs and factors. The text correctly refuses to derive perturbed actuator gradients from sup error alone. The integrated damping includes at least 4M, so the construction openly contains M commanded slots; no efficient aggregate computation is proved.

The compact-support quotient obstruction is valid for the proposed bounded linear reaction shortcut: a nonzero nonnegative smooth compact bump cannot satisfy Delta p<=C*p globally. The strong minimum principle applied on a connected domain containing its support and an exterior zero would contradict nontriviality. This says nothing against the separately identified alternatives with tails, walls, or nonlinear reaction.

Finally, the exact nominal controlled signal vanishes outside its material support. On passive release it has one sign, and incompressible transport plus diffusion preserve mass while smooth solutions exist. Its L1 norm is therefore bounded by its initial packet mass. Pairing with the specified bounded detector weight proves the readout bound and overlapping-error limitation. These statements concern the nominal passive release and fixed-volume bounded-error detector, not all possible exporters.

## Remaining proof debt

Approved conclusions are conditional finite-horizon initialization arithmetic, a controlled compact scalar mode with a stated nonlocal primitive, and bounded limitations for specific passive readout and local-reaction shortcuts. An admissible effective completed field, numerical off-axis bounds, physical initialization, localized sensor implementation, finite-speed export, backreaction control, input-computed gates, and the uniform polynomial-bit-time SAT theorem remain unresolved. None of the reviewed calculations proves P=NP, P!=NP, or a general impossibility of fluid computation. The original goal must remain open.
