# Complexity research catalog and paper-candidate priorities

S3049 / E014 / E004, 2026-09-11. **There are several defensible research directions, but no demonstrated general P-versus-NP breakthrough and no certified new paper theorem yet.** This is a selective catalog of 26 topic families, not 26 active projects or a claim that all major open questions are near resolution.

- [Classical/algebraic/proof catalog: C01-C14](2026-09-11-complexity-open-questions-classical.md): P/NP, NP/coNP, circuits, derandomization/PIT, VP/VNP, space, fine-grained complexity, average-case hardness, proof systems and meta-complexity.
- [Quantum catalog: Q1-Q12](2026-09-11-complexity-open-questions-quantum.md): quantum/classical classes, quantum witnesses, qPCP, restricted simulation, sampling hardness, quantum fine-grained assumptions, computation-tree costs and QAOA tails.
- [Independent claims/status review](2026-09-11-complexity-open-questions-review.md).

Both branches link primary sources and distinguish recent results from remaining open questions. Their proposed smaller questions are our suggestions, with novelty unverified. An accepted theorem, a conditional implication, a numerical observation and a proposed research question have different evidentiary roles. Catalog inclusion authorizes no proof campaign, experiments or publication.

Priority denotes usefulness to this program, not estimated probability of resolving a famous conjecture. Projects 2-6 require a focused novelty gate and a new execution story; they are not permission to run six parallel campaigns. Project 1 reuses S3048, the existing proposed, ready cost-accounting step; this catalog does not mark it executed. An unfavorable cost result can still be valuable if it identifies a precise bottleneck, but publishability is not guaranteed by a negative outcome either.

## How these connect to P versus NP

A uniform deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for general SAT would prove P=NP. A bounded-error polynomial quantum SAT algorithm would instead establish NP containment in BQP; an additional appropriate deterministic simulation is needed to conclude P=NP. A classical randomized simulation generally lands in BPP. A lower bound for a fixed proof system, restricted circuit model, oracle or geometric representation does not automatically prove P!=NP.

The older Horn, Hodge and holographic attempts are useful as checks on specific representations and missing effective operations; their failures do not exhaust all mathematics or all quantum algorithms. Entanglement and teleportation likewise do not by themselves specify a SAT-solving operation with bounded preparation, precision and readout costs. The present productive question is which exact operation supplies a demonstrable computational gain under an explicit resource model.

For every proposed paper, require: (1) a one-sentence new theorem, counterexample or empirical question; (2) exact input/model/quantifiers; (3) the closest full-text primary result and the difference; (4) a falsifiable acceptance criterion; and (5) a bounded plan with negative results retained. Established barriers and solved neighboring results in the two catalogs are guardrails for that selection, not additional unsolved targets.
