## Strongest supported candidate statement

**On a metadata-frozen set of 1,415 paired, source-reported SAT instances at n=20, r=176.54, the p60 mean inverse-square-root overlap is 2.6923 times the inverse square root of mean overlap, with a formula-bootstrap 95% interval [2.3970, 3.0362]; the corresponding p14 ratio is 1.7237 [1.6281, 1.8378].**

This is the strongest single empirical contribution statement presently supported. It quantifies one finite-data amplification-work proxy discrepancy. It is not a new inequality, a population-tail theorem, a discovery that the source authors overlooked tails, or a revised forecast of fault-tolerant advantage. The exact numerical calculation may be new to this audit, but publication priority for that calculation remains unverified. The local preregistered factor-two cutoff is a chosen materiality criterion, not a universal theoretical boundary.

The n16 independent simulations and chosen-construction counts can serve as separate illustrative case studies. They do not establish a causal or quantitative chain from this n20 distributional result to the source's p623/n179 crossover. A title or abstract claiming that the combined record disproves QAOA advantage would be unsupported.

## Alignment with the nearest source claims

[Boulebnane-Montanaro, PRX Quantum5,030348 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.030348), SecIII B/Fig2 and SecIII C/Fig4, already distinguish inverse mean success from median performance and discuss p60 median divergence and substantial instance variability. Therefore the S3044 ratio is a different calculated functional, not evidence that this phenomenon went unnoticed. The p60 mean inverse-root value6.41508 is still lower than p14's9.70559: the larger Jensen penalty does not erase every benefit of deeper preparation. Nor does the cutoff crossing establish that the true median is misestimated by that same factor.

[Omanakuttan et al., arXiv:2504.01897v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01897), equations7-11 and methods, already charge repeated preparation/inverse, marking/reflections, synthesis and fault-tolerant resources. Their abstract explicitly concerns a compiled QAOA+AA resource model and a classical heuristic crossover. We cannot claim to have discovered that these costs exist. The arXiv landing page checked on2026-09-11 lists v1 only; a literature check is not proof that no correction exists elsewhere.

The source model uses a mean-success-derived exponent and a median classical fit; a matched-statistic audit is legitimate. But multiplying a reported large-n runtime by the finite n20 Jensen ratio is not legitimate: it crosses both a statistic boundary and unverified size/depth/ensemble transfers. The source's explicitly incomplete witness-search setting also cannot be rejected merely because it is not a general SAT/UNSAT decision algorithm.

## Exact limits and disposition

The evidence supports neither a population lower-tail law nor an arbitrary-SAT algorithm, complexity separation, fault-tolerant speedup/refutation, source-runtime reproduction, novel compilation method or optimality theorem. A pessimistic implementation does not prove that every implementation fails. A unitary-only negative result does not rank total runtime when classical verification and readout vary. Stronger randomized or classical simulation implications require their own complexity assumptions.

**HOLD the combined publication candidate; retain the evidence as a reproducibility and model-audit record.** The strongest empirical statement is the finite archive statistic above.
