## What is elementary and where a contribution could remain

Once the source-specific moment identity is known, the fixed-character signed functional on any available t-row marginal has the explicit form

    [N E_uniform - (evaluation_at_all_plus + evaluation_at_all_minus)/2] / (N-1).

This follows immediately by Fourier coefficients: its constant coefficient is one, its odd moments zero, and its positive even moments -1/(N-1). A constant-negative Gram block is then a rank-one perturbation of the identity. Including all degrees separates it into even and odd blocks, each with that same simple form. Such extensions are immediate consequences of the already-derived identity and established finite-dimensional linear algebra; a fuller parity table alone does not cross a meaningful novelty threshold.

Likewise, the lower-window method is a general elementary perturbation argument: compare orthonormal characters under a base law with locally conditioned moments; bound each entry error by the conditioning failure probability; multiply by the matrix dimension to bound its operator norm. It is not a new positivity-preserving extension method. The substantive extra information is that the *entire X-only functional* retains positivity below the same asymptotic scale where the explicit witness fails. Unlike the parity-block extension, this addresses every polynomial in a substantially larger space and supplies a lower bound on any X-only obstruction.

No inspected theorem is an immediate named corollary that already states this source-specific two-sided scale. Conversely, the ingredients are elementary enough that unpublished recognition by the original authors is plausible. The correct description is an elementary analysis of a published functional with a potentially useful precise conclusion; neither a priority assertion nor a declaration of rediscovery is supported.
