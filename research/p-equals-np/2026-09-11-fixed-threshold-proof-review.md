I independently verified the complexity reviewer's zero-cylinder base argument. Let M=2^q and suppose at most 2^H truth tables have q-input circuit size at most T. If a separator depends on d table coordinates, all 2^(M-d) tables that equal zero on those coordinates receive its all-zero-table output. Provided the zero table is YES, that output must accept. Soundness against NO therefore implies every such completion has size at most T, giving M-d<=H. A one-output fan-in-two circuit with g gates has at most g+1 distinct input variables in its output cone, hence g>=M-H-1. The proof permits arbitrary behavior in the promise gap and does not assume low-complexity extensions of arbitrary partial tables. It also shows nonempty NO when M>H. Exact constants and the stopping dimension await the main.

The double-NOT padding consultation also checks under its explicitly limited syntactic rules: constant propagation, identical-input simplification and deletion of unreachable gates do not remove a reachable unary NOT chain whose input remains nonconstant. A promise-preserving restriction with nonempty YES and NO leaves the separator nonconstant. Arbitrarily long double-NOT tails consequently defeat a fixed fractional shrinkage bound for every syntactically padded separator. This observation does not apply to a simplifier including double-negation cancellation, nor to minimum-size separators; it is not a universal restriction obstruction.

Finally, a fixed-L,T recurrence extending through all arbitrarily large q would contradict an elementary nonuniform upper bound. Enumerate descriptions of all q-input circuits of size at most floor(T); accept a table if it equals one of their truth tables. This separates the stated gap in at most 2^q (q+T)^{O(T)} gates, with constants for the fixed basis. For fixed T this is 2^q times a polynomial in q, incompatible with a persistent strict superlinear exponential lower recurrence from a positive fixed base. A bounded slab with T tied to the original n is a different quantifier and is not refuted by this observation.
