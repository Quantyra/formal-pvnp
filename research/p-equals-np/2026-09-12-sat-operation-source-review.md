## Promise and implication contract

Valiant--Vazirani concerns the promise of zero versus one satisfying assignment, allowing arbitrary behavior on inputs with multiple satisfying assignments. It also covers finding the witness of a uniquely satisfiable instance. Its randomized isolation reduction preserves unsatisfiability and isolates a witness with inverse-polynomial probability. A polynomial-time promise solver therefore gives NP=RP, not a deterministic P=NP conclusion. This is established prior art. The distinct total language asking whether a formula has exactly one witness must not be conflated with the promise problem. [Original primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(86)90135-0); [author-hosted primary PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/tss/files/papers/1-s2.0-0304397586901350-main.pdf).

Operational audit, expanded here: require a polynomial time cap on each trial, including off-promise inputs. A search routine can behave arbitrarily on zero/multiple-witness formulas because every returned assignment is checked against the original formula before acceptance. If success on each unique instance is bounded below by an inverse polynomial, independent trials amplify the product of isolation and search success. On UNSAT there is never a verified witness. A two-sided promise decision routine can instead be amplified on all zero/one formulas queried during self-reduction; conditioned on successful isolation, each restricted formula retains at most one witness. Final witness verification restores one-sided soundness even when other trials fall outside the promise. These are conditional wrappers, not candidate algorithms.

An RP SAT procedure can fail on SAT and cannot certify UNSAT merely because a timeout produced no witness. Repeating a witness-only procedure until success yields expected polynomial time on satisfiable inputs but need not halt on UNSAT; that is not ZPP decision. ZPP requires zero error and expected polynomial termination on every input, including UNSAT. Deterministic P requires a uniform polynomial total-time bound and correctness on every input. Neither RP nor ZPP may be silently renamed P.

## One current source family checked, no selected bridge

The author's proposed source family is Basu--Hsieh--Lin--Manohar, *Solving Random Planted CSPs Below the n^{k/2} Threshold*, ICALP 2026. Theorem 4 is explicitly distributional: constraints are drawn from a planting law around an arbitrary fixed hidden assignment. It gives time n^O(ell), with a density threshold depending on planting distribution complexity. Its mechanism obtains an approximate planted assignment through SoS and then rounds further. Growing ell improves allowable density by increasing runtime. The constant-ell case does not establish a new worst-case polynomial SAT solver. [Primary theorem and algorithm](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.23/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.23.html).

A current closely related baseline is Kothari's June 2026 *Kikuchi Graphs of Random Hypergraphs are Approximately Johnson*. It proves spectral approximation using blocks of Johnson eigenspaces and applies it to planted noisy even-XOR SoS integrality at the stated random-hypergraph density. This is relevant follow-on source coverage for the same family, not a nominated second operation. [Primary report](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/099/).

The missing bridge is an executable transformation of each arbitrary satisfiable formula into the required planted law, without its witness already being known, with a polynomially detectable success event and witness decoding. Random variable permutations, sign masks, duplicate constraints or satisfiability-preserving gadgets do not automatically produce this law. A new transformation needs its own distribution theorem, size bound and decoding guarantee. No such bridge is selected by this review.
