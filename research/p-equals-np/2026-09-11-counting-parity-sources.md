# Counting and parity: focused primary-source comparison

Date: 2026-09-11. S3060, E004/S008. Independent source/adversarial note under README and INTEGRITY-CLAIMS. No implementation, simulation, publication or general SAT claim.

## Closest established interaction

**Gocht and Nordstrom, [Certifying Parity Reasoning Efficiently Using Pseudo-Boolean Proofs](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/16494/16301), AAAI 2021.** Section 4, Eq. (15), encodes parity as `sum x_i = b + 2 sum y_i` using fresh Boolean extension variables. Eqs. (19)–(22) use division and rounding to derive a reason clause for a parity-inconsistent assignment. The same section describes combining encoded parities and recovering short parity constraints from their CNF encodings; the latter translation explicitly uses extensions beyond ordinary cutting planes. Section 5 supplies a worked proof. Thus mixing integer counting and parity, and using rounding for parity consequences, already has concrete proof machinery. This source does not promise that arbitrary useful constraints can be discovered efficiently from arbitrary CNF.

**Elffers and Nordstrom, [Divide and Conquer: Towards Faster Pseudo-Boolean Solving](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/0180.pdf), IJCAI 2018.** Sections 1–3 distinguish stronger inference from efficient search and develop division-based conflict analysis. The input is a pseudo-Boolean constraint system, with ordinary CNF as a special case. The discussion explicitly identifies growing integer coefficients and the larger search space as costs. Its solver result is not a theorem that every counting or parity interaction has a polynomial deterministic discovery policy.

Access scope: the named primary sections, equations and surrounding explanations were inspected in full text. This is a targeted source comparison, not an exhaustive survey or claim to reproduce the complete implementations. The parity source is pinned to the AAAI 2021 proceedings version. No plain-cutting-planes simulation of a proposed hybrid follows merely from the fact that extensions can encode its parity operations.

## Elementary interaction to check exactly

If a Boolean cardinality sum s has proven integer bounds L<=s<=U and proven parity s congruent b modulo 2, its bounds can be tightened to the smallest integer at least L and the largest integer at most U of parity b. If the tightened interval is empty, the constraints are inconsistent. This is sound elementary arithmetic; treating it as a new inference principle would be unwarranted. For signed literals, normalize complements and adjust both the integer offset and the parity constant. For general weighted sums, cardinality-specific formulas cannot be copied without checking the coefficients.

To justify such an interaction from ordinary CNF, the counting bound and parity must both have explicit sound derivations on the same expression. Finding a maximum clique of mutually exclusive literals, the best cover, or a hidden parity is not a free step. Explicitly scanning a restricted candidate list and accepting only directly checkable certificates is different from assuming those objects are supplied or optimally found.

## Selected CNF discovery rule and exact comparison

The author fixes the following effective discovery scheme. At each sweep form the graph whose edges are negative binary clauses in the current unit-simplified residual. Original longer clauses can contribute new edges after shortening. For every edge uv, propose the vertex set consisting of u, v and all common neighbors; accept it as an at-most-one (AMO) constraint only if every pair is an edge. Scanning all edges and checking every candidate costs O(v^4) elementary adjacency work, before input-reading and bit/index overhead. This is a restricted deterministic candidate generator, not a maximum-clique oracle. The graph can change in at most v assignment-changing phases; within a phase newly learned positive ALO clauses cannot create negative edges. Thus the supports are not a globally fixed input list. The rule can miss other useful AMOs.

Seed affine equations only by inspecting exact variable-support buckets of width at most three in the current residual CNF. Evaluate each such bucket's relation; accept its equations only when the entire relation is affine. The implementation retains the original clauses, including learned positive ALOs, rather than replacing nonlinear relations by their hulls. Then use Gaussian reasoning, ordinary propagation, and these interactions on accepted AMO supports: even parity forces all variables false; odd parity yields an at-least-one clause; an AMO with a known at-least-one constraint gives exactly-one and hence odd parity. Same-support checks and generated constraints must be charged and canonicalized. The main source precedent above covers the underlying counting/parity arithmetic; no new generic inference rule is asserted.

For the author's positive family, take X={x1,...,xk}, k>=4, and Y={y1,y2}. Supply pairwise AMO CNFs for X and Y, the two cover clauses `(x1 OR y1)` and `(x2 OR y2)`, and a standard short-XOR CNF chain for XOR(X)=0. The clique generator recovers both AMOs. The short support buckets and Gaussian elimination recover even parity on X. Combining it with AMO(X) yields X=0; the cover clauses then force y1=y2=1, contradicting AMO(Y).

This derivation is a same-input comparison against two **specified restricted policies**. The parity-only policy has no initial unit assignment or counting conversion. The counting-only policy applies its prescribed aggregate test: sum all positive clauses and all accepted AMOs, and compare only proportional coefficient vectors. Here the positive covers omit x3,...,xk while the AMO aggregate includes them, so this test does not close. This is not a separation from all parity inference, all pseudo-Boolean reasoning, cutting planes, or more capable case learners.

The functional pigeonhole regression is ordinary counting: recovered pigeon-row and hole-column AMOs cover each cell twice, while the positive row clauses cover each cell once. Scaling the lower bound by two contradicts the sum of the upper bounds. It checks this implementation's discovery and aggregation; it is not a new pigeonhole proof.

## A structurally different elementary limitation

The source scout proposed the following finite example to the author, as an analytic check rather than a new experiment. On five variables, for every three-element subset include its positive clause and its all-negative clause. Each triple must have both Boolean values, impossible because one value occurs at least three times among five variables.

Nevertheless there are no negative binary edges, so the selected graph rule discovers no AMO. Each exact triple bucket is the not-all-equal relation on three bits, whose six satisfying assignments have full affine hull. It yields no affine equation. There are no unit clauses; the selected interactions have no starting AMO or XOR to combine. Thus this rule has an unresolved fixed point on an explicit UNSAT input. The all-positive aggregate alone does not contradict a bound; even adding the symmetric raw negative aggregate would only give 5/3<=sum(x)<=10/3. This is a limitation of the selected extraction and inference rules, not all counting/parity methods. Originality of the formulation is unverified.

## Component and lower-bound boundaries

Known counting proofs of pigeonhole do not make a new counting-plus-parity mechanism. Conversely, a lower bound for a restricted counting system does not automatically apply after adding Gaussian elimination, extension variables, case learning or new rounding rules. A comparison must name the exact component policies, including whether they share learned assignments and whether their extraction routines already expose the relevant relations.

An additional trap is exactly-one constraints: exactly one true variable implies odd parity on the same support. A sufficiently capable parity extraction policy can therefore obtain that relation without the proposed counting feedback. A demonstration that counting is necessary must be against the frozen specified component, not all parity reasoning. Likewise a pseudo-Boolean component with the relevant extension encoding can already express parity interactions. These are scope checks, not reasons to avoid testing an explicit restricted mechanism.
